Additionally, the Threat Actors (TAs) responsible for these attacks often use a double extortion technique, where they encrypt the files and exfiltrate sensitive data from the victim’s device before encryption. These TAs then leverage this stolen data to extort their victims further by threatening to release it on a leaked site unless their demands are met.  </p> <p>The TAs are constantly devising new methods to extort money from their victims. In the previous year, Cyble Research and Intelligence Labs (CRIL) discovered a ransomware variant that not only encrypts victims’ files but also steals their Discord tokens. </p> <div id="om-wlravxmwms40sunr2q0v-holder"></div>  <p>Recently, CRIL spotted a new strain of <a class="wpil_keyword_link" href="https://cyble.com/knowledge-hub/what-is-malware/" title="malware" data-wpil-keyword-link="linked" data-wpil-monitor-id="510" target="_blank">malware</a> known as the “BlackSnake” ransomware that is capable of performing clipper operations aimed at cryptocurrency users. This variant was initially identified by a researcher <a href="https://twitter.com/siri_urz/status/1630571985653702661?s=20" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">@siri_urz</a>. It was detected in the cybercrime forum in 2022, and the TAs behind it were actively seeking affiliates. </p> <p>In addition, the TAs claimed they would take a 15% share of the profits generated Beyond Traditional Tactics with Clipper Integration        Ransomware is a significant threat that can encrypt its victims' files and demand a ransom. Additionally, the Threat Actors (TAs) responsible for these attacks often use a double extortion technique, where they encrypt the files and exfiltrate sensitive data from the victim's device before encryption. These TAs then leverage this stolen data to extort their victims further by threatening to release it on a leaked site unless their demands are met.&nbsp;&nbsp;    The TAs are constantly devising new methods to extort money from their victims. In the previous year, Cyble Research and Intelligence Labs (CRIL) discovered a ransomware variant that not only encrypts victims' files but also steals their Discord tokens.\u00a0    Recently, CRIL spotted a new strain of malware known as the \"BlackSnake\" ransomware that is capable of performing clipper operations aimed at cryptocurrency users. This variant was initially identified by a researcher @siri_urz. It was detected in the cybercrime forum in 2022, and the TAs behind it were actively seeking affiliates.&nbsp;    In addition, the TAs claimed they would take a 15% share of the profits generated through the affiliation process, as shown in the figure below.&nbsp;    Figure 1 \u2013TAs advertisement in Cyber Crime Forum&nbsp;        Our analysis has uncovered evidence suggesting that BlackSnake Ransomware has been created based on the source of Chaos ransomware. In this blog, we delve into the technical aspects of BlackSnake Ransomware, including its clipper operations.&nbsp;    Technical Analysis        Static analysis of the sample with hash: e4c2e0af462ebf12b716b52c681648d465f6245ec0ac12d92d909ca59662477b shows that the malicious file is a 32-bit PE binary compiled using .NET, as demonstrated in the following figure:&nbsp;    Figure 2 \u2013File Information&nbsp;        Upon execution, the BlackSnake Ransomware performs an initial check to verify if the current input language of the system matches the language codes \"az-Latn-AZ\" or \"tr-TR\".&nbsp;&nbsp;    If a match is found, the ransomware immediately terminates itself, indicating that the TAs of BlackSnake ransomware intend to exclude systems located in Azerbaijan or Turkey from being infected, as shown below.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;    Figure 3 \u2013 Locale Check&nbsp;    After confirming the user\u2019s location, the BlackSnake Ransomware creates a registry entry, as shown below.&nbsp;&nbsp;     HKEY_CCURRENT_USER\\SOFTWARE\\oAnWieozQPsRK7Bj83r4&nbsp;     The BlackSnake ransomware has a method of detecting whether it has already infected a system. It does this by checking the location of the executing assembly with the path \"C:\\Users\\AppData\\Roaming\\svchost.exe\". If this path matches, the ransomware continues to search for the file named \"UNLOCK_MY_FILES.txt\" in the %appdata% directory. Once the file is found, the ransomware will terminate itself. This behavior suggests that the ransomware is designed to avoid infecting a system more than once, and it may be an attempt to limit the impact of the ransomware.&nbsp;&nbsp;    The below figure shows the code snippet used by the malware for validation.&nbsp;    Figure 4 \u2013 File Path Validation\u202f    &nbsp;To prevent multiple instances of the malware from running concurrently, the malware enumerates the names of all currently running processes, retrieves the filename of the current executing assembly, and compares it with the filenames of the running processes. If there is a match, the malware then compares the Process ID of the current process with that of the target process.
