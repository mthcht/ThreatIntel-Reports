                <div class="blog-post-content" id="blog-post-content">
                    <div class="blog-post-content__section--text"><p><strong>Fake investment <a href="https://www.group-ib.com/resources/knowledge-hub/scam/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">scams</a> have been around for long enough so that people could recognize them easily. For instance, in 2020, Group-IB Digital Risk Protection Team detected a massive bitcoin <a href="/media-center/press-releases/singapore-bitcoin-scam-campaign/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">scam campaign</a> in Singapore which used the names of local celebrities. Nevertheless, no matter how old the scheme is, it keeps bringing money to fraudsters.</strong></p>
<p>The Group-IB <a href="https://www.group-ib.com/services/cert/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Computer Emergency Response Team</a> (CERT-GIB) <strong>has been investigating several fake investment schemes which are targeting European citizens</strong>. In the course of the research, a gigantic network infrastructure was uncovered and analyzed, which contained over 10,000 rogue resources, including similar fraudulent ones aimed at the inhabitants of the entire Eurasian continent and North America.</p>
<p>In our research, we noticed that the following countries are being targeted with the Fake investment scheme:</p>
<ul>
<li>UK</li>
<li>Belgium</li>
<li>Netherlands</li>
<li>Germany</li>
<li>Poland</li>
<li>Portugal</li>
<li>Norway</li>
<li>Sweden</li>
<li>Czech Republic</li>
</ul>
<p>The main goal of these <strong>fake investment schemes is to convince the victims to repeatedly transfer funds to the fake investment portal</strong>. The victims are usually promised huge returns on their investments and are shown “how I got rich” stories featuring celebrities.</p>
<p><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-20543" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/image.webp" alt="phishing website screenshot" width="1244" height="703" /></p>
<p>In this post, <strong>Reza Rafati</strong> from the <a href="https://www.group-ib.com/services/cert/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Group-IB Computer Emergency Response Team</a> (CERT-GIB) in Amsterdam will take a deeper dive into the fake investments schemes, showcase a couple of them including <strong>a conversation with the scammers</strong> that we managed to record, and provide recommendations for the users. The aim of this research is to <strong>raise awareness about the fake investment scheme and ultimately reduce the number of victims</strong>. We encourage cybersecurity researchers and the general public to join the fight against cybercrime and share fraudulent domains with us via “Report an Incident” form at our website for further evaluation and takedown.</p>

<p>Legitimate looking investment sites are popping up everywhere. We see them on various social media networks, which include the massive platforms of Youtube and Facebook.</p>
<p>The message displayed on those platforms makes it seem like there is a bulletproof service of making an online income. The messages state that the service is used by famous people globally. This can be from Elon Musk to local Dutch and UK celebrities. The message continues to state that it is a unique offer and that you just need a minimal deposit of 250 euros to get started.</p>
<div id="attachment_20535" style="width: 551px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-20535" decoding="async" class="wp-image-20535 size-full" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/image-1-1.webp" alt="fake investment schemes - screen from Facebook" width="541" height="623" /><p id="caption-attachment-20535" class="wp-caption-text">Screenshot from a rogue Facebook profile which is sharing the scheme</p></div>
<p>The scam disseminates deceptive information as though ‘<em>Gert Verhulst</em>’ has surprised experts and is frightening the banks, all because he made an investment. The <strong>fake investment scheme illegally exploits the name and the image of this famous person</strong> to lure the victims to click on the link. Gert Tony Hubert Verhulst is a Belgian media entrepreneur, television producer, presenter, actor and singer. He is known in the Netherlands for his television role in the show &#8216;<em>Samson en Gert</em>&#8216;.</p>
<p>Additionally, the victim will leave a comment below the hyperlink, stating that with <strong>250 euros</strong> the victim was able to have a profit of <strong>700 euros</strong> in just 3 days.</p>
<p>In some cases, specially created rogue Facebook pages were being used to spread the fake investment scheme post via the advertisement capabilities on Facebook.</p>
<div id="attachment_20536" style="width: 514px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-20536" decoding="async" class="wp-image-20536 size-full" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/image-2-1.webp" alt="fake investment schemes - screen from Facebook message luring victims" width="504" height="647" /><p id="caption-attachment-20536" class="wp-caption-text">A promoted Facebook message luring victims to the fake investment schemes</p></div>
<p>The following footage is taken from Youtube, in this video the fraudster is trying to lure users to contact him. Once contacted by the victims, the scammer will assist the victims in creating the needed profile to start with the investment, and also assist in getting the initial minimal 250 euro deposit.</p>
<p>A <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f2rbRax8X1c" rel="nofollow">video</a> on Youtube trying to lure the viewers into contacting the actor that is participating in the fake investment schemes.</p>
<p><img decoding="async" class="alignnone wp-image-20544 size-full" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/image8.gif" alt="fake investment schemes - screen from youtube" width="874" height="619" /></p>
<p><strong>The ultimate goal is to lure the victims into visiting fake investment portals.</strong></p>
<h2>Your personal account manager</h2>
<p>The scammers behind this scheme try to maximize their impact and set up the same scheme with different templates that cover specific topics. Below you can see a collection of the templates seen by CERT-GIB in the fake investment schemes.</p>
<p>The fake sites make use of templates that seem to be of high quality. The color palettes used and the code itself makes it look like a legitimate and trustworthy website.</p>
<p><strong>The main idea of the fake broker sites is to show content to the victims which will trigger them to:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Register an account</li>
<li>Replenish the balance on their previously made account</li>
<li>Bombard the victims with success stories</li>
<li>Draw fictitious stories that profit is being made via the fake broker sites</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>The scammers social engineer the victim into making an investment.</strong> Once the victim lands on the fake broker site, they will see various fake messages of people that have had “successful” trades and are in the process of cashing out. The fake broker site will for example state that a random name from your city just has withdrawn a couple of hundred euros.</p>

<p>The final page where the victim is requested to perform a transaction of 250 EUR or more. It is at this stage where the credit card details are compromised and the victim loses money.</p>
<h2>How we got scammed (on purpose)</h2>
<p>This scheme makes use of several steps to social engineer the victim into sending out money. <strong>For research and awareness-building purposes we signed up with one of those scam websites.</strong> Shortly after signing up, we received a phone call.</p>
<p>During this phone call the scammers asked several questions that you would expect when dealing with a legitimate investment service:</p>
<ul>
<li>Why do you want to invest?</li>
<li>How much can you invest?</li>
<li>How do you make your money?</li>
<li>How much time do you spend on work?</li>
</ul>
<p>On each answer of these questions the scammer gave a positive response which led to the next step in the call. At the end of the call, the fraudster insisted on giving payment card details immediately. He was very upset when we refused. Remember, the sense or urgency that the scammers are often trying to induce is one of the red flags. Refer to the recommendations section below for more tips.</p>

<h3>In a nutshell, the scheme looks as follows:</h3>
<ol>
<li><strong>The scammer puts a fake investment scheme post via a (compromised) social media account</strong> on a social media network like Facebook or Youtube.</li>
<li>The victim sees the fake investment post and is lured into clicking the post as the post claims that easy profit can be made with a minimal deposit of 250 EUR.</li>
<li>The victim clicks on the post which translates itself into two steps:<br />
a. The victim is navigated directly to the fraudulent scheme site or an “advertisement” site which shows the “profit” famous people are making with the investment plan.<br />
b. The victim is forwarded to an fraudulent investment site which shows successful projects and income plans.</li>
<li>The victim is shown a form which asks for contact details.</li>
<li>Once the form is filled, the victim will be contacted by phone by one of the call centers that is being used for this scheme.</li>
<li>During the call, the scammer will try to social engineer the victim into believing that the investment plan is a legitimate plan. It is also in this step that the scammer informs the victim that with a minimum of 250 euro deposit with a credit card the investment can be started.</li>
<li>Once the victim has made a deposit of 250 euro or more, the victim will get a login to a fake investment dashboard.</li>
<li>In the fake dashboard profit will be shown (while literally there is no profit as all is fake), the scammers do this to be able to ask the victim for more money, as the victim believes good profits are being made. No actual trading is taking place on the platform.</li>
<li>The scammer (fake account manager) continues to ask for more deposits so that the victim can increase their profits. If the victim agrees to deposit more, the victim will be sent back to step 8. If the victim denies, and wants to cash out, the victim will be shown step 10.</li>
<li>The scammer will state that the victim needs to put another deposit to meet the payout threshold. If the victim agrees to do this, they will be sent to step 8 again, if the victim refuses to deposit more, the victim will quickly realize that they have participated in a fake investment scheme and that the money has been lost.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The scale of fake investment schemes</h2>
<p>We used our patented <a href="/products/threat-intelligence/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Graph Network Analysis</a> tool within the <a href="/products/threat-intelligence/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Threat Intelligence</a> system and it quickly showed us the immense size of the schemes that have been setup.</p>
<p><strong>At the moment of writing, CERT-GIB tracked down 11,197 domains that were used and included in the general fraudulent infrastructure targeting Europe &#8211; this included chains of redirects and hosts of fraudulent content.</strong></p>
<p><strong>The lifetime of fraudulent sites varied from several days to several months</strong> (the more victims the site brought, the faster it went offline and its mirror was raised on another domain). At the moment of writing, from those <strong>11,197</strong> domains, <strong>5,091</strong> still remain active.</p>
<div id="attachment_20542" style="width: 1047px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-20542" decoding="async" class="wp-image-20542 size-full" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/image-8.webp" alt="fake investment schemes - graph view on one of the domains" width="1037" height="744" /><p id="caption-attachment-20542" class="wp-caption-text">A graph view on one of the domains &#8211; leading to a massive forest of fraudulent investment scheme sites. <p>The fraudsters make use of specific keywords and top level domains to trick unaware internet users into their fraudulent scheme. The keywords range from specific investment categories like Bitcoin and gold, but this is not where they stop, they continue to set up schemes that target specific countries.</p>
<p>In our research, we noticed that <strong>the following countries are being targeted with the Fake investment scheme</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>UK</li>
<li>Belgium</li>
<li>Netherlands</li>
<li>Germany</li>
<li>Poland</li>
<li>Portugal</li>
<li>Norway</li>
<li>Sweden</li>
<li>Czech Republic</li>
</ul>
<h2>Recommendations</h2>
<p><strong>The earning techniques used in the schemes abuse the trust of the victims</strong>, the scammers perform <a href="/resources/knowledge-hub/social-engineering/">social engineering</a> methods to keep the victim hooked.</p>
<p>In order to protect yourself and your surrounding against these type of threats, we have the following recommendations for you:</p>
<ul class="checks">
<li>Investments can often be done via legitimate and established brokers. There are many sites that provide detailed information about these legitimate brokers. So don’t simply click and join a site via an advertisement, do your own research online and validate that you are dealing with a legitimate broker website.</li>
<li>When you start with investments, you don’t need to have a personal account manager telling you what to do by phone. Keep your money in your pocket and educate yourself in regards to investing.</li>
<li>Search for reviews &#8211; the established brokers will have reviews. These reviews can help you to get a better understanding of the service and the quality that is provided.</li>
<li>Often the fake broker domains have a short lifespan, this means that the domains that have been registered are quite new.</li>
<li>You can report suspicious domains at our website via “Report an Incident” form. Our team will evaluate and take down fraudulent websites upon confirmation.</li>
<li>Financial organizations are recommended to use <a href="/products/fraud-protection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Group-IB’s Fraud Protection</a> to stop fraudulent activity even after its customers share payment details with the scammers.</li>
<li>Group-IB <a href="/products/digital-risk-protection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Digital Risk Protection</a> defends the personal brand of individuals by hunting down impersonations and illegal copies of accounts.</li>
</ul>
