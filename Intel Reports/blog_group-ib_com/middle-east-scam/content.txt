                <div class="blog-post-content" id="blog-post-content">
                    <div class="blog-post-content__section--text"><h2>Introduction</h2>
<p>In spring 2021, Group-IB <a href="/products/digital-risk-protection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Digital Risk Protection</a> (DRP) analysts identified <strong>a fraud scheme targeting users in Arabic-speaking countries</strong>. As part of their attacks, <strong>the threat actors abused more than 130 well-known brands</strong> worldwide from sectors such as telecommunications, retail, entertainment, and etc.</p>
<p>In total, Group-IB analysts discovered more than<strong> 4,300 fraudulent pages</strong> <strong>created on Blogspot</strong>, a popular blogging service. The webpages were all registered by a group that included more than 100 accounts.</p>
<p>The scammers used a tried-and-tested scheme involving giveaways supposedly by popular brands, lottery games purporting to be recommended by celebrities, and fake job offers from the government. The threat actors used such lures to steal personal data or attract traffic to other fraudulent websites. More than <strong>500,000 people</strong> per month visit the end websites involved in the scheme.</p>
<h2>How the scheme works</h2>
<p>Users fall victim to the schemes by agreeing to take part in a promotion supposedly organized by a famous brand, a government organization, or a celebrity. Victims are promised they could win a prize or money, play the &#8220;wheel of fortune&#8221;, or get a job by completing a survey.</p>
</div>

<p>Victims may be asked to enter their full names, phone numbers, places of residence, education details, and desired place of work.<br />
Regardless of the answers or where the wheel of fortune stops, victims become &#8220;winners.&#8221; After completing the survey or playing the wheel of fortune, victims are asked to share the link to the website or a similar one with a lottery game with 5-20 WhatsApp contacts. The scammers do so to widen the pool of their potential victims.</p>
<p>After the victim sends the required number of messages, they are redirected to another fraudulent resource: other lottery games, scam dating websites, and websites with fake browser extension installations. In the worst-case scenario, victims may end up on a malicious or phishing website.</p>
<div id="attachment_25546" style="width: 522px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/4-min-14.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-25546" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-25546 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/4-min-14.png" alt="Fraudulent Blogspot page where the victim is asked to share the lottery game with their WhatsApp contacts" width="512" height="448"></picture><p id="caption-attachment-25546" class="wp-caption-text">Fraudulent Blogspot page where the victim is asked to share the lottery game with their WhatsApp contacts</p></div>
<p><strong>Telecommunications companies have been the main target for threat actors.</strong> Fraudsters have abused at least <strong>47 telecom brands</strong> as part of their scheme. In addition to the telecommunications sector, brands in the retail, entertainment and automotive sectors have been affected.</p>
<div id="attachment_25547" style="width: 1510px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/5-min-14.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-25547" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-25547 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/5-min-14.png" alt="the distribution of brands affected in the scam infographics" width="1500" height="843"></picture><p id="caption-attachment-25547" class="wp-caption-text">Affected industries</p></div>
<p>In addition to company brands, scammers abused personal brands of public figures, especially the Saudi royal family.</p>
<p>The fraudulent campaign targeted 16 Arabic-speaking countries: <strong>Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Jordan, Sudan, Morocco, Egypt, Bahrain, Iraq, Yemen, Palestine, the United Arab Emirates (UAE), Algeria, Lebanon, Qatar, Syria, and Oman</strong>. The attack also targeted English-speaking users from Turkey and Nigeria.</p>
<p><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/6-min-11.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-25548 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/6-min-11.png" alt="the distribution of brands affected in the scam infographics" width="1500" height="843"></picture></p>
<p><strong>The fraudsters did not always use popular brands or celebrities on their websites</strong>. Group-IB DRP specialists also discovered fake dating websites and fake lottery games.</p>
<p>To lure users to scam websites, the fraudsters sent bulk WhatsApp messages and used pop-up windows and Google Ads. The first Blogspot account related to the fraudster group was registered in August 2013. Account registrations peaked in 2018 and the threat actors continued creating new accounts in 2019 and 2020. To this day, on some of the accounts the fraudsters continue to create fraudulent pages that abuse many brands.</p>
<p><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/7-min-12.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-25549 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/7-min-12.png" alt="the timiline for registration of scam accounts on blogpost" width="1500" height="843"></picture></p>
<h2>How the threat group operates</h2>
<p>Using Blogspot service as part of their schemes is typical for the threat group in question.</p>
<div id="attachment_25550" style="width: 769px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/8-min-11.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-25550" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-25550 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/8-min-11.png" alt="Fraudulent Blogspot page where victims are asked to share a link to the survey with their WhatsApp contacts" width="759" height="1074"></picture><p id="caption-attachment-25550" class="wp-caption-text">Fraudulent Blogspot page where victims are asked to share a link to the survey with their WhatsApp contacts</p></div>
<p>In addition to registering fake pages that mimic the websites of well-known brands, the scammers use Blogspot as a data storage space or a content delivery network (<strong>CDN</strong>) to store media content and webpage code. In some cases, such data is uploaded to individual domains, which allows the fraudsters to save on hosting services.</p>
<p>The scammers can also use Blogspot as a link shortening service and redirect users to fraudulent domains. Search engines consider such links safe and do not show any warnings about the website being potentially dangerous.</p>
<p>It is difficult to detect the page from which visitors are redirected, especially when it comes to regular users, because the redirection is instant and users simply do not notice that they have been redirected.</p>
<div id="attachment_25551" style="width: 1510px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/9-min-11.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-25551" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-25551 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/9-min-11.png" alt="How fraudulent Blogspot pages work scheme" width="1500" height="843"></picture><p id="caption-attachment-25551" class="wp-caption-text">How fraudulent Blogspot pages work</p></div>
<h2>Attack attribution</h2>
<p>Finding links between elements of the fraudster group&#8217;s infrastructure is relatively easy. In addition to identical names (51.9% of the profiles had &#8220;od.company&#8221; in their names), the registered accounts interlink each other and use the same servers, groups of domains, and links for sharing on WhatsApp. All the above factors mean that it is highly likely that the profiles belong to a single threat group.</p>
<p>The diagram below shows a high-level example of grouping these accounts by traffic source, Google statistics, and connections between domains.</p>
<p><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/10-min-10.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-25552 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/10-min-10.png" alt="diagram of a high-level example of grouping scam accounts" width="1625" height="856"></picture></p>
<p><strong>The threat group uses more than 100 accounts, and that number keeps growing.</strong> For instance, over the first six months of this year, we have observed a 54% surge in the number of pages on these accounts. The earliest accounts related to the threat group were registered in 2013, which is surprising because fraudulent pages are usually active for no longer than a few months. Yet this group has been operating for at least six years.</p>
<p>In addition to Blogspot, <strong>the threat group uses many other tools, including social media ads and mass messaging in messaging apps.</strong></p>
<p><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/11-min-6.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-25542 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/11-min-6.png" alt="scam blogpost page" width="883" height="809"></picture></p>
<h2>How to avoid falling victim to fraud</h2>
<ol>
<li>Be cautious while following links that allegedly lead to the website of a specific company, a celebrity or a state agency and trust links from the official resources only — verified accounts on social media or messengers.</li>
<li>Enter confidential data and bank card details on trusted websites only.</li>
<li>When visiting links relating to offers by companies shared in messaging apps or on social media, check the domain names. Fraudsters usually use domain names that sound similar to brand names.</li>
<li>Verify the information about promotions and giveaways on the official accounts of brands, state agencies or celebrities.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Recommendation for rightsholders</h2>
<ol>
<li>Monitor and analyze complaints of the company&#8217;s customers who fell victim to scammers.</li>
<li>Monitor online resources to promptly detect the illegal use of the company&#8217;s name or trademark.</li>
<li>To prevent the illegal use of your intellectual property assets, use <a href="/products/digital-risk-protection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Digital Risk Protection</a> (DRP) solutions that help promptly detect threats to a specific brand in the online space and then send them for blockage.</li>
</ol>

    function positioningSidebar() {
        let sidebar = document.getElementById('sidebar');
        let sidebarHeight = sidebar.offsetHeight;
        let windowHeight = document.documentElement.clientHeight;
        if (windowHeight < (sidebarHeight + 48)) {
            let sidebarAuthors = sidebar.querySelector('.sidebar__authors');
            let sidebarMorePosts = sidebar.querySelector('.sidebar__more-posts');
            let authorsHeight = sidebarAuthors ? sidebarAuthors.offsetHeight : 0;
            let morePostsHeight = sidebarMorePosts ? sidebarMorePosts.offsetHeight : 0;
            let sidebarTop = authorsHeight + morePostsHeight + 140;
            jQuery(sidebar).css('top', -sidebarTop + 'px');
        } else {
            jQuery(sidebar).css('top', '72px');
        }
    }
    window.addEventListener('resize', positioningSidebar);
    positioningSidebar();

    if (localStorage.getItem('theme')) {
        if (localStorage.getItem('theme') == 'dark') {
            jQuery('body').addClass('theme--dark');
        } else if (localStorage.getItem('theme') == 'light') {
            jQuery('body').addClass('theme--white');
        }
    } else {
        localStorage.setItem('theme', 'dark');
        jQuery('body').addClass('theme--dark');
    }

    jQuery('.theme-toggle').click(function() {
        if (localStorage.getItem('theme') == 'dark') {
            localStorage.setItem('theme', 'light');
        } else {
            localStorage.setItem('theme', 'dark');
        }
        jQuery('body').toggleClass('no-transition');
        jQuery('body').toggleClass('theme--dark');
        jQuery('body').toggleClass('theme--white');
        setTimeout(() => {
            jQuery('body').toggleClass('no-transition');
        }, 250);
    });
    jQuery('.share-toggle').click(function() {
        jQuery('.share-toggle').toggleClass('active');
        jQuery('.share-widget').toggleClass('show');
    });
