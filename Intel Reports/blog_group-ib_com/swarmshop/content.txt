                            <h1 class="header__title title-h2 mb-24 mt-md-48 mt-lg-64 mt-xl-64">When Karma Comes Back</h1><p class="header__text mb-xs-48 mb-64 mb-lg-96 mb-xl-96">The rise and fall of illicit cardshop breached twice in two years</p><div class="post-info__date mb-24 mb-lg-32 mb-xl-48"><div class="body2">April 8, 2021 · <span id="reading-time"></span> min to read · Threat Intelligence</div></div><div class="post-info__image mb-24 mb-lg-32 mb-xl-48"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/img-1440-29.jpg.webp" type="image/webp"><img src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/img-1440-29.jpg" alt="" class="webpexpress-processed"></picture></div><div class="post-info__tags"><div class="post-info__tag body2">Cardshop</div><div class="post-info__tag body2">Swarmshop</div><div class="post-info__tag body2">Threat Intelligence</div></div>                        </div>
                    </div>
                </div>
                <div class="blog-post-content" id="blog-post-content">
                    <div class="blog-post-content__section--text"><p>Group-IB <a href="/products/threat-intelligence/">Threat Intelligence</a> system found that user data of the <strong>Swarmshop</strong> <a href="/resources/knowledge-hub/card-shop/">card shop</a> has been leaked online on March 17, 2021. The database was posted on a different underground forum and contained 12,344 records of the card shop admins, sellers and buyers including their nicknames, hashed passwords, contact details, history of activity, and current balance. In addition to user data, <strong>the database exposed all compromised data traded on the website</strong>, including 623,036 payment card records, 498 sets of online banking account credentials and 69,592 sets of US Social Security Numbers and Canadian Social Insurance Numbers.</p>
<p><strong>While underground hacker forums get hacked from time to time, cardhsop breaches do not happen very often.</strong> In addition to buyers&#8217; and sellers&#8217; data, such breaches expose massive amounts of compromised payment and personal information of regular users. Although the source remains unknown, it must be one of those revenge hacks cases. This is a major reputation hit for the illicit cardshop as all the sellers lost their goods and personal data. The cardshop is unlikely to restore its status</p>
<p>Group-IB Threat Intelligence unit took a deep dive into the history of illicit cardshop, examined the driving forces behind its rise and fall, and discovered that the underground marketplace was breach twice in just two years by fellow cybercriminals.</p>
<h2>Swarmshop profile</h2>
<p><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/1-min-18.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-26101 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/1-min-18.png" alt="swarmshop website screenshot" width="992" height="124"></picture></p>
<p><strong>Swarmshop &#8211; is an underground mid-size &#8220;neighborhood&#8221; store for stolen personal and payment records.</strong> The cardshop has been operating since at least 2019, as the resource was first mentioned on underground forums in April 2019.</p>
<p>The user nicknamed &#8220;Swarmshop&#8221; is likely a cardshop administrator as they post information about updating databases and answers user questions on various forums under different nicknames. It is also known that cardshop admins are Russian-speaking.</p>
<p>By March 2021, it had more than 12K user base and over 600K payment card records on sale. The total amount deposited on all the accounts was at $18,145.73 – users of card shops do not store large amounts of money on their accounts and top up the balance to make payments if necessary.</p>
<p>This information was obtained from the data exposed in the March 2021 breach. Interestingly, it was not the first time Swarmshop has been targeted by fellow hackers. In January 2020, the cardshop&#8217;s records were leaked on an underground forum.</p>
<h2>Swarmshop database leak in January 2020</h2>
<p>On January 19, 2020, a user of the &#8220;procrd&#8221; forum, likely motivated by revenge, posted a message with an offer to sell the Swarmshop database.</p>
<div id="attachment_26102" style="width: 1506px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/2-min-23.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-26102" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-26102 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/2-min-23.png" alt="message with an offer to sell the Swarmshop database" width="1496" height="435"></picture><p id="caption-attachment-26102" class="wp-caption-text">Source: Group-IB Threat Intelligence</p></div>

<h2>Swarmshop database leak in March 2021</h2>
<p>On March 17, 2021, a newly-registered user posted a link and password from the database of the &#8220;Swarmshop&#8221; market on the &#8220;crdclub&#8221; forum.</p>
<div id="attachment_26105" style="width: 807px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/5-min-25.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-26105" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-26105 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/5-min-25.png" alt="Screenshot from the "crdclub" forum" width="797" height="260"></picture><p id="caption-attachment-26105" class="wp-caption-text">Screenshot from the &#8220;crdclub&#8221; forum</p></div>
<p>On March 18, 2021, a user with similar nicknames posted the same link and password on &#8220;tor&#8221; and &#8220;procrd&#8221; forums and added one sentence: &#8220;leak of cc shop swarmshop&#8221;.</p>
<p>It should be noted that in the case of all three accounts that posted these messages, they were the user&#8217;s first and only messages on the forums.</p>
<p>The post on procrd is currently deleted from the forum. Nevertheless, Group-IB Threat Intelligence system was able to retrieve the original posts.</p>
<div id="attachment_26106" style="width: 1506px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/6-min-18.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-26106" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-26106 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/6-min-18.png" alt="swarmshop post on procrd" width="1496" height="421"></picture><p id="caption-attachment-26106" class="wp-caption-text">Source: Group-IB Threat Intelligence</p></div>

<h2>Analysis of information exposed in March breach</h2>
<p>The analysis of the database found the structured data ﬁles subdivided into two categories:</p>
<ul>
<li>Detailed compromised payment and personal records traded on the cardhsop</li>
<li>Information about cardshop admins, sellers, and buyers.</li>
</ul>
<p>Swarmshop users in the database are divided into three groups: &#8220;Admin&#8221; (website administrators and moderators),&#8221;Seller&#8221; (accounts belonging to sellers who post data for sale), and &#8220;Buyer&#8221; (accounts belonging to regular users).</p>
<p>Each account is assigned one of four statuses. &#8220;Active&#8221; (valid account without restrictions) on the one hand, and &#8220;Archive&#8221;, &#8220;Blocked&#8221;, and &#8220;Suspended&#8221; on the other (presumably accounts with various kinds of restrictions to access the site).</p>
<p>The analysis of the freshly exposed database found that the information was new as it indicated the latest user activity timestamps. Considering that the most recent date in the database is March 10, 2021 08:18:36, it can be assumed that the data was downloaded between 8:18 and 9:00 on March 10. However, the time zone cannot be deduced from the ﬁles.</p>
<p>In total,<strong> the databased revealed the records of 4 cardshop admins, 90 sellers, and 12,250 buyers of stolen data</strong>, including their nicknames, hashed passwords, account balance, and contact details for some entries. The share of various statuses are shown in the table below.</p>

Users of card shops do not store large amounts of money on their accounts and top up the balance to make payments if necessary.</p>
<p>While the source of the breach remains unclear, the exposed records show that two card shop users attempted to inject a malicious script searching for website vulnerabilities in the contact information field. It&#8217;s impossible to determine if the two events are connected to the breach.</p>
<p><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/18-min-8.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-26118 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/18-min-8.png" alt="a malicious script searching for swarmshop website vulnerabilities " width="1077" height="291"></picture></p>
<p>Compromised payment and personal records in the database were divided into three types:</p>
<ul>
<li>CC &#8211; full bank card details (number, expiration date, owner name, etc.)</li>
<li>VBA &#8211; login and password for online bank accounts or payment systems</li>
<li>SSN &#8211; Social Security Number (also contained SIN – Social Insurance Numbers in Canada)</li>
</ul>
<p>The dump contained <strong>623,036</strong> payment card records, <strong>62.7 percent</strong> of which were issued by the US banks. Other records were issued by the financial institutions from China (14.02%), the UK (3.24%), Canada (3.09%), France (3.07%), Singapore (1.6%), Brazil (1.32%), Saudi Arabia (0.99%), and Mexico (0.86%). Group-IB notified the national CERTs in the abovementioned countries about the breach so they could take the necessary steps to mitigate the threat.</p>
<p><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/19-min-1.jpg.webp" type="image/webp"><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-26119 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/19-min-1.jpg" alt="distribution of compromised swarmshop payment records diagram" width="1498" height="843"></picture></p>
<p>In addition to stolen bank cards, <strong>the database revealed 498 sets of online banking account credentials and 68,995 sets of US Social Security Numbers and 597 pieces of Canadian Social Insurance Numbers.</strong></p>
<p>Companies that process user personal and payment information need to ensure the integrity and security of customer data by putting in place advanced <a href="/products/threat-intelligence/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Threat Intelligence</a> solutions which allow to monitor for leaked credentials. Constant underground monitoring for compromised personal and payment customer records, gives banks and financial organisations the ability to mitigate risks and further damage by quickly blocking stolen cards.</p>
<h2>Swarmshop growth: 2019 &#8211; 2021</h2>
<p>Based on the data obtained from the leaks that occurred in 2020 and 2021, it is possible to analyze how the market developed over the years.</p>
<p>For example, &#8220;Seller&#8221; accounts increased by 36 (90 in total compared to 54 at the time of the ﬁrst leak). The number of &#8220;Buyer&#8221; accounts grew more than 2.5 times (from 4,757 to 12,250). It is worth clarifying, however, that the number of discovered accounts is only a total number and does not reﬂect the number of users who were active on the resource. Another parameter using which we were able to compare the two leaks is the unspent funds on &#8220;Buyer&#8221; accounts: $8,242 during the leak in 2020 and as much as $18,145 during the data breach in March, 2021.</p>
<p>The net proﬁt of the Swarmshop administrators at the time of the ﬁrst leak was about $1,300 per month, while in 2021 it is fair to assume that it was as much as $5,300 per month. The change is due to an increase in the total number of &#8220;Buyer&#8221; accounts and fees for Swarmshop administration services increasing from 20% to 30%.</p>
<p><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/20-min-1.jpg.webp" type="image/webp"><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-26120 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/20-min-1.jpg" alt="swarmshop growth 2019-2021" width="1498" height="843"></picture></p>
<h2>Recommendations for financial organisations</h2>
<ul>
<li>Notify users of possible risks in the online payment process when using bank cards.</li>
<li>If payment cards related to your bank have been compromised, block these cards and notify the users as soon as possible.</li>
<li>Receive first-hand reports about compromised payment and personal records sales on the Dark web. Check for the cards issued by the bank in the DBs for sale.</li>
</ul>
