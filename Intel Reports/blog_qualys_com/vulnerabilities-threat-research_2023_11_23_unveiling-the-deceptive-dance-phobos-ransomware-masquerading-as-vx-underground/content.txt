<p>During a recent hunt, Qualys Threat Research has come across a ransomware family known as Phobos, impersonating VX-Underground. Phobos ransomware has been knocking on our door since early 2019 and is often seen being distributed via stolen Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) connections. Strongly believed to be closely tied to the preceding Dharma malware, Phobos usually operates as a Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) threat model.</p>



<h2 id="about-vx-underground" class="wp-block-heading">About VX-Underground</h2>



<p>VX-Underground is an open-source community with the largest collection of malware source code, samples, and papers on the internet.<br>VX-Underground is the most popular source among the threat research community to share malware samples across the globe.</p>

<p><strong>AntiRecuvaAndDB.exe (763b04ef2d0954c7ecf394249665bcd71eeafebc3a66a27b010f558fd59dbdeb)</strong></p>



<p>The sample is being distributed with a masqueraded name (AntiRecuvaAndDB.exe) of a legitimate software suite known as Recuva, which is a very popular data recovery software. <p>The ransomware tries its best in order inhibit the system recovery by means of executing the following commands: </p>



<p><strong>Delete Shadow Copy</strong></p>



<p>vssadmin delete shadows /all /quiet</p>



<p>wmic shadowcopy delete</p>



<p><strong>Disables automatic Windows Recovery by modifying boot configuration data</strong></p>



<p>bcdedit /set {default} bootstatuspolicy ignoreallfailures</p>



<p>bcdedit /set {default} recoveryenabled no</p>



<p><strong>Delete Windows Backup Catalog</strong></p>



<p>wbadmin delete catalog -quiet</p>



<p><strong>Disable Windows Firewall</strong></p>



<p>netsh advfirewall set currentprofile state off</p>



<p>netsh firewall set opmode mode=disable</p>

<p><strong>.id[unique_id].[staff@vx-underground.org].VXUG</strong></p>
