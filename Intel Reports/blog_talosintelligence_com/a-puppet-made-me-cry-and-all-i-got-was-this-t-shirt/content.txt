    <section class="post-content-wrapper mt-5">
        <div class="post-content" id="post-content">
            <p>Welcome to this week’s edition of the Threat Source newsletter.&nbsp;</p><p>Anyone who&nbsp;spoke with&nbsp;me&nbsp;in&nbsp;the&nbsp;last several weeks has had to deal with me&nbsp;loudly waiting in&nbsp;anticipation for&nbsp;the long-awaited&nbsp;“Project Hail Mary”&nbsp;movie adaptation.&nbsp;I read&nbsp;(and&nbsp;cried over)&nbsp;the book by Andy Weir,&nbsp;who’s&nbsp;also the author of&nbsp;“The Martian,”&nbsp;about a year ago and,&nbsp;shortly&nbsp;after,&nbsp;found out it was&nbsp;being made into&nbsp;a movie.&nbsp;</p><p>(I know what&nbsp;you’re&nbsp;thinking: Two movie-themed editions in two weeks?&nbsp;It’s&nbsp;every cinephile’s dream!)&nbsp;</p><p>Anyway, the story centers around a biologist and science&nbsp;teacher named Ryland Grace&nbsp;(Ryan Gosling), who wakes up&nbsp;from a coma&nbsp;on a&nbsp;spaceship lightyears&nbsp;away from Earth, his two crewmembers long dead.&nbsp;Our planet’s sun is slowly&nbsp;dimming,&nbsp;its energy being&nbsp;consumed&nbsp;by alien microbes&nbsp;called “astrophage”&nbsp;that&nbsp;are infecting all the stars&nbsp;in our stellar&nbsp;neighborhood&nbsp;—&nbsp;except one. Grace’s task&nbsp;is to figure out why this&nbsp;star is unaffected and send the solution back to Earth.&nbsp;It's&nbsp;a one-way trip, and&nbsp;he’ll&nbsp;eventually&nbsp;die&nbsp;in space alone...&nbsp;or so&nbsp;he&nbsp;thinks.&nbsp;</p><p>The movie&nbsp;met 99.9% of my expectations, which is rare for an adaptation.&nbsp;The humor was spot-on,&nbsp;the soundtrack was gorgeous,&nbsp;and the puppetry&nbsp;—&nbsp;yes, the&nbsp;<a href="https://youtube.com/shorts/lQrNFU2w9Xw?si=kVwyBBCeXHWBBV2Z" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>puppetry</u></a>&nbsp;(mild&nbsp;spoilers&nbsp;for Rocky, Grace’s new alien friend)&nbsp;—&nbsp;was out-of-this-world.&nbsp;</p><p>While&nbsp;it&nbsp;<em>is</em>&nbsp;a story about space,&nbsp;it’s&nbsp;first and foremost&nbsp;about communication, trust, and collaboration&nbsp;—&nbsp;things&nbsp;we’re&nbsp;no strangers to at Talos, especially&nbsp;when creating the&nbsp;Year in Review&nbsp;report&nbsp;(which&nbsp;<a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/2025yearinreview" rel="noreferrer noopener"><strong><u>is</u></strong>&nbsp;<strong><u>available now</u></strong></a>).&nbsp;The entire processof creating this report, from raw data to final design, is only a little bit less monumental than stopping alien microbes from&nbsp;plunging the earth into an ice age.&nbsp;</p><p>The process begins with Talos’ Strategic Analysis team, who&nbsp;leverage&nbsp;the vast amount of Cisco’s telemetry, Talos research, and&nbsp;data from Talos Incident Response cases to analyze trends over the past year. This analysis is synthesized into a comprehensive report, which undergoes rigorous review and proofing at multiple levels. While the report is being drafted, the Strategic Comms team develops a detailed schedule of content and collateral to promote it both internally and externally, meeting weekly to track our progress. Once the text is&nbsp;finalized, it moves to our design team, who transform the data into a visually stunning, accessible format. Even after the report launches,&nbsp;the&nbsp;work continues:&nbsp;We produce videos,&nbsp;<a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/cybersecurity/comments/1s46l9v/were_cisco_talos_ask_us_anything_24h_ama/" rel="noreferrer"><u>answer your questions on Reddit</u></a>&nbsp;(today only!),&nbsp;record podcasts, create social media graphics,&nbsp;and&nbsp;collaborate across Cisco&nbsp;to ensure our findings reach the right people.&nbsp;</p><p>We do this for the good of the community. Our report&nbsp;isn’t&nbsp;gated, and it never will be; you can read it right in your browser without filling out&nbsp;fake names and emails in&nbsp;annoying forms. Talos’ job is to keep as many people as safe as possible, and that means free access to critical information.&nbsp;Here's&nbsp;a taste of our findings:&nbsp;</p><ul><li>React2Shell was the&nbsp;No.&nbsp;1 most targeted CVE in 2025 despite&nbsp;only&nbsp;being&nbsp;discovered&nbsp;in&nbsp;December.&nbsp;ToolShell&nbsp;was&nbsp;No.&nbsp;3 despite being released in June.&nbsp;</li><li>About 25% of the vulnerabilities on our top 100 list affect widely used frameworks and libraries, highlighting the risk of supply chain-style attacks.&nbsp;</li><li>Nearly a&nbsp;third of MFA spray attacks targeted identity and access management (IAM) applications.&nbsp;</li><li>Attackers continued to rely heavily on phishing for&nbsp;initial&nbsp;access,&nbsp;observed&nbsp;in 40% of Talos IR cases.&nbsp;35% of cases involved internal phishing.&nbsp;</li><li>Qilin&nbsp;was the most seen ransomware variant in 2025,&nbsp;with over 40 victims each month except January.&nbsp;</li></ul><p>We also offer insights on AI and state-sponsored threats, so be sure to&nbsp;<a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/2025yearinreview" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>view the full report</u></a>.&nbsp;</p><p>In&nbsp;“Project Hail&nbsp;Mary,”&nbsp;Grace&nbsp;and his&nbsp;alien&nbsp;friend, Rocky,&nbsp;realize that they&nbsp;can't&nbsp;save their respective worlds alone.&nbsp;The Talos Year in Review is the result of a massive, cross-functional mission. It takes&nbsp;collaboration between all of Talos’ teams to turn complex, often daunting telemetry into actionable intelligence&nbsp;for the community.&nbsp;</p><p>When we share knowledge, communicate clearly, and work together, the results are,&nbsp;to quote Rocky,&nbsp;“Amaze! Amaze! Amaze!”&nbsp;</p><p>Stay tuned over the coming days and weeks as&nbsp;we break each section down into the most important&nbsp;2025 Year&nbsp;in Review findings&nbsp;you need to know.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/content/images/2026/03/Screenshot-2026-03-26-at-10.06.49---AM.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="2000" height="668" srcset="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/content/images/size/w600/2026/03/Screenshot-2026-03-26-at-10.06.49---AM.png 600w, https://blog.talosintelligence.com/content/images/size/w1000/2026/03/Screenshot-2026-03-26-at-10.06.49---AM.png 1000w, https://blog.talosintelligence.com/content/images/size/w1600/2026/03/Screenshot-2026-03-26-at-10.06.49---AM.png 1600w, https://blog.talosintelligence.com/content/images/size/w2400/2026/03/Screenshot-2026-03-26-at-10.06.49---AM.png 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><h2 id="the-one-big-thing">The one&nbsp;big thing&nbsp;</h2><p>One of the main themes from the&nbsp;<a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/2025yearinreview/" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>2025</u>&nbsp;<u>Year in Review's</u></a>&nbsp;vulnerability data is that attackers are targeting identity by compromising the infrastructure that sits around it, including physical hardware devices, software, and management platforms.
