    <h1 class="text-center">Don’t swing at everything</h1>

    <div class="text-center m-1">
        <div class="post-author">
            <span>By </span>
                <a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/author/thorsten/">Thorsten Rosendahl</a>
        </div>

        <br/>
        <time class="post-datetime" datetime="July 23, 2026 14:00">
            Thursday, July 23, 2026 14:00
        </time>

        <div class="m-3">
                        <a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/category/threat-source-newsletter/" class="category primary-category">
                            Threat Source newsletter
                        </a>
                            </div>
    </div>

    <section class="post-content-wrapper mt-5">
        <div class="post-content" id="post-content">
            <p>Welcome to this week’s edition of the Threat Source newsletter.&nbsp;</p><p>Lately I've found myself thinking a lot about the Australian TV series Mr. Inbetween (IMDb 8.7/10) — not because I'm a hitman for hire, but because I literally feel in-between. Specifically, in-between what I'd call the "pre-Mythos" and “post-Mythos” eras. We've crossed a capability threshold, and it's not just one model family driving that — Codex 5.3 and GPT-5.5 deliver <a href="https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-evaluation-of-openais-gpt-5-5-cyber-capabilities" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>comparable or better performance</u></a>, and <a href="https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/chinas-360-says-it-has-developed-tools-match-anthropics-mythos-2026-06-24/" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>Tulongfeng</u></a> or <a href="https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.2" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>GLM-5.2</u></a> (an incredibly powerful open-weight model, MIT-licensed) show the frontier isn't limited to closed models anymore.&nbsp;</p><p>On the other side of that line, real-world impact hasn't caught up yet and we're living in an artificial buffer zone. For me, defining the “pre-” and “post-” status comes down to the gap between "vulnerability discovery" and "vulnerability publication."&nbsp;</p><p>Last week’s Patch Tuesday gave a signal of change, as Joe <a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/begun-the-patch-wars-have/" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>pointed out</u></a>, so maybe the buffer zone has come to an end.&nbsp;</p><p>Let's dive into the 2026 Q2 stats.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/07/blog_CVEline.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="2000" height="1178" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/07/blog_CVEline.png 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1000/2026/07/blog_CVEline.png 1000w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1600/2026/07/blog_CVEline.png 1600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w2400/2026/07/blog_CVEline.png 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><p>As in past years, we're seeing a steeper curve than the year before — a solid 49% YoY growth, though still not the hockey-stick moment I keep waiting for. By the end of June we were tracking close to 200 CVEs per day.&nbsp;</p><p>Using the keyword methodology described <a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/patch-track-repeat-the-2025-cve-retrospective/" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>here</u></a>, I found 452 AI-related CVEs this calendar year. If "openclaw" is added to the keyword list, that number jumps by another 536 — a reminder that these counts are sensitive to keyword drift. Given how much the keyword list keeps changing, I'm reconsidering whether to keep publishing this particular metric going forward.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/07/blog_KEVline.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="2000" height="1178" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/07/blog_KEVline.png 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1000/2026/07/blog_KEVline.png 1000w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1600/2026/07/blog_KEVline.png 1600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w2400/2026/07/blog_KEVline.png 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><p>KEVs, by contrast, "only" grew 13% — a small April spike aside, it's fairly flat relative to total CVE growth. Networking-gear-related CVEs continued their climb, now accounting for 24% of KEV-related vulnerabilities (up from 20% in Q1) — consistent with the trend I flagged last quarters.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/07/blog_pie.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="2000" height="792" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/07/blog_pie.png 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1000/2026/07/blog_pie.png 1000w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1600/2026/07/blog_pie.png 1600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w2400/2026/07/blog_pie.png 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><p>As in previous quarters, CVEs from 2024 or earlier still make up about 24% of everything we're tracking. More strikingly, even though the standard enterprise patch cycle is described&nbsp; to run 30–90 days, 181 days into 2026, 46% of today's actively-exploited (KEV) CVEs still trace back to 2025 or earlier.&nbsp;</p><p>Old vulnerabilities don't retire, new ones keep arriving, and machine-speed vulnerability discovery is going to keep outpacing human-speed patching. Which brings me back — once again — to <a href="https://www.first.org/epss/" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>EPSS</u></a> as a tool for prioritizing patching against this dataset.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/07/EPSS_table.jpg" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="2000" height="791" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/07/EPSS_table.jpg 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1000/2026/07/EPSS_table.jpg 1000w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1600/2026/07/EPSS_table.jpg 1600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w2400/2026/07/EPSS_table.jpg 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><p>If you patched purely by CVSS 9+, you'd be urgently chasing ~3,700 CVEs — but 95% of those sit below 5% EPSS, meaning the real-world odds of exploitation are tiny.&nbsp;</p><p>Of the 32 CVSS 9+ CVEs with EPSS ≥ 50%, 25 are already on CISA's KEV list. The remaining seven outliers are still high-probability by EPSS but haven't made KEV yet — worth watching.&nbsp;</p><p>Ray Shoesmith (Mr. Inbetween) once told his therapist, "You know, if I hit somebody, I generally got a pretty good reason."&nbsp;</p><p>Same principle applies to patching. Don't swing at everything — swing at what you have good reason to believe is coming for you.&nbsp;</p><h2 id="the-one-big-thing">The one big thing&nbsp;</h2><p>Cisco Talos has <a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/chaos-msarat-living-off-the-browser-to-build-covert-c2-channel" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>discovered "msaRAT,"</u></a> a new Rust-based remote access trojan (RAT) deployed by the Chaos ransomware group. Built on the Tokio asynchronous runtime, it establishes a covert command-and-control (C2) channel by hijacking Chrome or Edge browsers via the Chrome DevTools Protocol (CDP). The infection starts with a deceptive MSI file masquerading as a Windows update that loads the payload directly into memory, paving the way for ransomware deployment.&nbsp;</p><h3 id="why-do-i-care">Why do I care?&nbsp;</h3><p>This RAT is a master of evasion, living off the browser to build its C2 infrastructure without ever directly touching the network. By routing traffic through legitimate browser processes and trusted services, msaRAT easily bypasses traditional network-based detections. Additionally, its use of the Tokio runtime enables highly efficient, parallel execution of malicious tasks, accelerating the attacker's ability to establish persistence and deploy double-extortion ransomware.&nbsp;</p><h3 id="so-now-what">So now what?&nbsp;</h3><p>Defenders should monitor for unusual&nbsp;curl&nbsp;commands, especially those downloading MSI files to the&nbsp;ProgramData&nbsp;directory or sending plain HTTP traffic over port 443. Scrutinize unexpected MSI files impersonating Windows updates and watch for unauthorized Chrome or Edge manipulation. Finally, implement behavioral monitoring to catch Chrome DevTools Protocol abuse and unauthorized WebRTC connections. <a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/chaos-msarat-living-off-the-browser-to-build-covert-c2-channel" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>Read the full blog</u></a> for complete coverage and indicators of compromise (IOCs).&nbsp;</p><h2 id="top-security-headlines-of-the-week">Top security headlines of the week&nbsp;</h2><p><strong>Introducing Antares: Highly efficient open weight AI models for vulnerability localization</strong>&nbsp;<br>This week, Cisco introduced Antares, a family of security small language models (SLMs) purpose-built for pinpointing where known vulnerabilities exist within a codebase. (<a href="https://blogs.cisco.com/ai/introducing-antares-the-most-efficient-open-weight-ai-models-for-vulnerability-localization" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>Cisco</u></a>)&nbsp;</p><p><strong>Hacker wipes European country’s entire land registry database, paralyzing real-estate market</strong>&nbsp;<br>A hacker wiped Romania’s entire land registry database after an unsuccessful extortion attempt. The attack halted all property transactions, preventing notaries from authenticating sales or registering mortgages nationwide. (<a href="https://cybernews.com/security/hacker-deletes-romanian-land-registry-database/" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>Cybernews</u></a>)&nbsp;</p><p><strong>"WP2Shell” opens millions of WordPress sites to remote takeover</strong>&nbsp;<br>Barely three days after disclosure, attackers are widely chaining together CVE-2026-60137 and CVE-2026-63030 to lob exploit attempts against one of the largest attack surfaces on the Internet. (<a href="https://www.darkreading.com/cyberattacks-data-breaches/wp2shell-millions-wordpress-sites-remote-takeover" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>DarkReading</u></a>)&nbsp;</p><p><strong>Progress tells ShareFile customers to shut down Storage Zone Controllers over security threat </strong>&nbsp;<br>Only the Storage Zone Controller is affected, not standard cloud-only ShareFile accounts.
