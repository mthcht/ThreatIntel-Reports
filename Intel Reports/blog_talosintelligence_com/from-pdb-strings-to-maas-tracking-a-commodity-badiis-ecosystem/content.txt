    <div class="text-center m-1">

            <ul><li>Cisco Talos has uncovered a BadIIS variant — identifiable by its embedded "demo.pdb" strings — that functions as commodity malware. This variant is likely sold or shared among multiple Chinese-speaking cybercrime groups that operate under a&nbsp;<a href="https://blog.talosintelligence.com/need-to-know-commodity-malware/" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>malware-as-a-service (MaaS)</u></a>&nbsp;model for continuous monetization.&nbsp;</li><li>Analysis of&nbsp;program database (PDB)&nbsp;file&nbsp;paths reveals a sustained, multi-year development effort by an author&nbsp;operating&nbsp;under the alias “lwxat”,&nbsp;spanning from at least September 2021 through January 2026, with evidence of rapid iterative updates, feature branching, and reactive evasion tactics targeting specific security vendors such as Norton.</li><li>Talos recovered a dedicated builder tool that allows threat actors to generate configuration files, customize payloads, and inject parameters into&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;binaries&nbsp;—&nbsp;enabling capabilities including traffic redirection to illicit sites, reverse proxying for search engine crawler manipulation, content hijacking, and backlink injection for&nbsp;malicious&nbsp;search engine optimization (SEO)&nbsp;fraud.&nbsp;</li><li>Beyond&nbsp;BadIIS, the same author has developed a suite of auxiliary tools&nbsp;—&nbsp;including service-based installers, droppers, and persistence mechanisms that automate deployment, ensure survivability across IIS server restarts, and evade detection through custom Base64 encoding and obfuscation techniques.</li></ul><hr><h2 id="mystery-badiis-containing-%E2%80%9Cdemopdb%E2%80%9D">Mystery&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;containing&nbsp;“demo.pdb”&nbsp;</h2><p>Since 2024, Talos has investigated&nbsp;numerous&nbsp;attacks across the Asia-Pacific region&nbsp;(along with a few in South Africa,&nbsp;Europe&nbsp;and North America)&nbsp;that&nbsp;utilize&nbsp;a specific variant of&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;characterized by "demo.pdb" strings. While multiple security vendors are tracking the global spread of these variants,&nbsp;Talos'&nbsp;observed&nbsp;tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs)&nbsp;show notable divergences from those documented by other vendors like&nbsp;<a href="https://www.trendmicro.com/en_us/research/25/b/chinese-speaking-group-manipulates-seo-with-badiis.html" rel="noreferrer">Trend Micro</a>,&nbsp;<a href="https://asec.ahnlab.com/jp/65289/" rel="noreferrer">Ahnlab</a>, VNPT,&nbsp;and&nbsp;<a href="https://www.elastic.co/security-labs/badiis-to-the-bone-new-insights-to-global-seo-poisoning-campaign" rel="noreferrer">Elastic</a>. Consequently, it is difficult to attribute these attacks to a single threat actor. However, we assess with&nbsp;moderate&nbsp;confidence that the "demo.pdb"&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;variant is a commodity tool&nbsp;utilized&nbsp;by multiple Chinese-speaking cybercrime groups.&nbsp;</p><h2 id="insights-from-embedded-pdb-strings">Insights from embedded PDB strings&nbsp;</h2><p>Although the core functionality of this&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;variant is&nbsp;largely limited&nbsp;to SEO fraud, content injection, and proxy‑based traffic manipulation, our investigation pivoted toward the&nbsp;malware’s&nbsp;embedded PDB strings.&nbsp;The consistent PDB path pattern offers much more intelligence value than the generic&nbsp;“demo.pdb”&nbsp;filename. Additionally, we recovered customization parameters and execution logs associated with this installer, which&nbsp;provided&nbsp;deeper insights into its overall capabilities.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig16.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="741" height="523" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/05/fig16.png 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig16.png 741w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"><figcaption><span style="white-space: pre-wrap;">Figure&nbsp;16.&nbsp;Customization parameters and execution&nbsp;logs&nbsp;file.</span></figcaption></figure><p>The commands and parameters embedded in the install are also encoded.&nbsp;Below&nbsp;is&nbsp;a list of the encoded strings and their decoded counterparts.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/tables2.jpg" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="2000" height="2158" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/05/tables2.jpg 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1000/2026/05/tables2.jpg 1000w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1600/2026/05/tables2.jpg 1600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w2400/2026/05/tables2.jpg 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><p>The secondary malware component functions similarly to the previously described service tool. However, recognizing that security operations centers (SOCs) or antivirus products can easily quarantine or delete the primary BadIIS malware, the author has implemented a robust persistence mechanism. The installer now copies the BadIIS malware not only to the active directory used for hooking IIS requests and responses but also to a hidden backup location. This ensures that the malicious BadIIS is automatically restored and launched every time the compromised IIS server is restarted. The table below provides a list of the encoded strings and their decoded counterparts.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/tables3.jpg" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="2000" height="1293" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/05/tables3.jpg 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1000/2026/05/tables3.jpg 1000w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w1600/2026/05/tables3.jpg 1600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w2400/2026/05/tables3.jpg 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><h3 id="module-initialization-dropper">Module initialization dropper&nbsp;</h3><p>Alongside the service-based tools, Talos identified another utility that shares the same C2 authentication mechanism, custom Base64 encoding algorithm, and similar code structure. However, rather than&nbsp;operating&nbsp;as a persistent service, this tool functions primarily as a dropper designed to install the&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;malware onto the target IIS server. The embedded PDB string (“D:\vc\dll封装进exe\x64\Release\moduleinit.pdb”,&nbsp;which translates to "DLL packaged into EXE") explicitly confirms its purpose: packaging malicious DLL payloads within a standalone executable. The&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;are&nbsp;found&nbsp;in the resource and named&nbsp;as “IIS32” and “IIS64” (see Figure 17).</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig17.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="859" height="244" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/05/fig17.png 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig17.png 859w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"><figcaption><span style="white-space: pre-wrap;">Figure&nbsp;17.&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;malware in the resource.</span></figcaption></figure><p>The drop location for this&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;malware is&nbsp;identical to the one used by the installation script&nbsp;previously documented by&nbsp;<a href="https://www.trendmicro.com/en_us/research/25/b/chinese-speaking-group-manipulates-seo-with-badiis.html" rel="noreferrer noopener"><u>Trend Micro</u></a>.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig18.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="665" height="849" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/05/fig18.png 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig18.png 665w"><figcaption><span style="white-space: pre-wrap;">Figure&nbsp;18.&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;malware drop location.</span></figcaption></figure><h2 id="lwxat-badiis-author-identification">"lwxat":&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;author&nbsp;identification&nbsp;</h2><p>Through&nbsp;detailed&nbsp;analysis of&nbsp;numerous&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;samples, associated tools, and builder artifacts,&nbsp;Talos&nbsp;assesses&nbsp;with moderate-to-high confidence that the string&nbsp;"lwxat"&nbsp;is&nbsp;the&nbsp;author's alias or handle. This assessment is based on the following converging evidence:&nbsp;</p><ul><li><strong>Builder</strong>&nbsp;<strong>authentication</strong>&nbsp;<strong>mechanism:</strong>&nbsp;The&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;builder and service tool uses the string&nbsp;"lwxat"&nbsp;as a hardcoded match string within its authentication routine,&nbsp;suggesting the author embedded their identity into the tool's access control logic.&nbsp;</li><li><strong>Configuration</strong>&nbsp;<strong>parameter:</strong>&nbsp;The string&nbsp;"lwxat"&nbsp;is used as the enable function parameter within the&nbsp;builder's&nbsp;“config.txt”&nbsp;file, further&nbsp;indicating&nbsp;authorship attribution embedded in the&nbsp;tool's&nbsp;operational configuration.&nbsp;</li><li><strong>User-agent signature: </strong>Most notably, several BadIIS malware samples were observed using "lwxatisme" as a custom user-agent string during HTTP communications — a strong behavioral indicator that directly ties the malware to the "lwxat" persona.</li></ul><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig19.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="566" height="155"><figcaption><span style="white-space: pre-wrap;">Figure&nbsp;19.&nbsp;The&nbsp;custom&nbsp;user-agent string “lwxatisme”.</span></figcaption></figure><p>Additionally, corroborating evidence was&nbsp;identified&nbsp;through&nbsp;PDB path strings&nbsp;found within certain samples. One PDB path&nbsp;contained&nbsp;the&nbsp;Chinese-language string:</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig20-1.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="665" height="68" srcset="https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/size/w600/2026/05/fig20-1.png 600w, https://storage.ghost.io/c/af/a0/afa04ee3-414f-4481-8d23-7e7c146f192e/content/images/2026/05/fig20-1.png 665w"></figure><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card kg-card-hascaption"><img for&nbsp;x神’s&nbsp;requirements.</span></figcaption></figure><p>This suggests that the author created a dedicated development folder for a user or client named&nbsp;"xshen" (x神),&nbsp;indicating&nbsp;that this particular&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;variant was a&nbsp;customized build&nbsp;tailored specifically for&nbsp;“xshen's”requirements that a full-site traffic hijacking with redirection logic based on the victim's browser language settings.</p><p>Collectively, these findings presence of&nbsp;"lwxat"&nbsp;across the builder's authentication, configuration, and in-the-wild&nbsp;user-agent strings, combined with the PDB path referencing a customized build for&nbsp;“xshen”&nbsp;and provide converging evidence indicating that&nbsp;"lwxat"&nbsp;is the primary developer or operator behind the&nbsp;BadIIS&nbsp;malware family, potentially offering&nbsp;customization services&nbsp;to other threat actors.&nbsp;</p><h2 id="coverage">Coverage&nbsp;</h2><p>The following&nbsp;ClamAV&nbsp;signatures detect and block this threat:&nbsp;</p><ul><li>Win.Malware.BadIIS-10059971-0&nbsp;</li><li>Win.Malware.BadIIS-10059977-0&nbsp;</li><li>Win.Malware.BadIIS-10059984-0&nbsp;</li><li>Win.Malware.BadIIS-10059985-0</li></ul><p>The following SNORT® rules (SIDs) detect and block this threat:&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><ul><li>Snort2: 1:66400, 1:66399, 1:66398&nbsp;</li><li>Snort3: 1:66400, 1:301491&nbsp;</li></ul><h2
