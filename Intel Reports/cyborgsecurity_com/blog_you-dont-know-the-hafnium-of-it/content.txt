</p><p><strong>Who Are They?</strong></p><p>On 2 March 2021, Microsoft released a <a class="underline decoration-2 decoration-red-600 underline-offset-2 break-words hover:decoration-red-700" aria-label="Link to https://www.microsoft.com/security/blog/2021/03/02/hafnium-targeting-exchange-servers/#scan-log" target="_blank" href="https://www.microsoft.com/security/blog/2021/03/02/hafnium-targeting-exchange-servers/#scan-log">blog</a> article detailing a new threat actor it had dubbed <strong>HAFNIUM</strong>. Microsoft, the blog identified, has observed the actor exploiting several 0-day vulnerabilities.</p><p>Microsoft also highlighted that the HAFNIUM group had previously targeted other organizations. The group, Microsoft identified, has focused on the exploitation of Internet-facing services in the past.</p><p>Later that same day, the company Volexity also released a <a class="underline decoration-2 decoration-red-600 underline-offset-2 break-words hover:decoration-red-700" aria-label="Link to https://www.volexity.com/blog/2021/03/02/active-exploitation-of-microsoft-exchange-zero-day-vulnerabilities/" target="_blank" href="https://www.volexity.com/blog/2021/03/02/active-exploitation-of-microsoft-exchange-zero-day-vulnerabilities/">blog</a> article. In their article, they identified that they observed the attacks beginning as early as 3 January 2021.</p><p>Hot on the heels of the Microsoft and Volexity blogs, other vendors began to contribute information. On 4 March 2021, FireEye&#x27;s Mandiant Intelligence release their own <a class="underline decoration-2 decoration-red-600 underline-offset-2 break-words hover:decoration-red-700" aria-label="Link to https://www.fireeye.com/blog/threat-research/2021/03/detection-response-to-exploitation-of-microsoft-exchange-zero-day-vulnerabilities.html" target="_blank" href="https://www.fireeye.com/blog/threat-research/2021/03/detection-response-to-exploitation-of-microsoft-exchange-zero-day-vulnerabilities.html">blog</a> post. In it, FireEye identified that they tracked the activity under three separate activity clusters:</p><ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 my-4 ml-4 xl:ml-6"><li class="">UNC2639</li><li class="">UNC2640</li><li class="">UNC2643</li></ul><p>One particularly important point in this blog article is that FireEye detected this activity all the way back in January 2021. The article also highlighted various TTPs and tools the actors used. This included:</p><ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 my-4 ml-4 xl:ml-6"><li class="">ASPXSPY</li><li class="">Covenant</li><li class="">China Chopper</li><li class="">Nishang</li><li class="">PowerCat</li><li class="">Cobalt Strike</li></ul><p>They also described the actors&#x27; method of deploying webshells on compromised Exchange servers. These webshells were unique though. The webshells detected the presence of specific security products and warn the actors. The security products it detected included FireEye, Carbon Black and Crowdstrike. They described the actors technique of dropping more complex webshells over time. This appears to be to avoid detection.</p><p>In time, other vendors have also come forward, including <a class="underline decoration-2 decoration-red-600 underline-offset-2 break-words hover:decoration-red-700" aria-label="Link to https://symantec-enterprise-blogs.security.com/blogs/threat-intelligence/microsoft-exchange-server-protection" target="_blank" href="https://symantec-enterprise-blogs.security.com/blogs/threat-intelligence/microsoft-exchange-server-protection">Symantec</a>. They identified that they track HAFNIUM, under the name <strong>Ant</strong>.</p><p> </p><p><strong>Where is HAFNIUM From?</strong></p><p>Attribution can be a tricky topic in cyber threat intelligence. Despite this fact, <a class="underline decoration-2 decoration-red-600 underline-offset-2 break-words hover:decoration-red-700" aria-label="Link to https://www.forbes.com/sites/daveywinder/2021/03/04/microsoft-exchange-attacks-are-declared-an-emergency-by-homeland-security/" target="_blank" href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/daveywinder/2021/03/04/microsoft-exchange-attacks-are-declared-an-emergency-by-homeland-security/">several</a> <a class="underline decoration-2 decoration-red-600 underline-offset-2 break-words hover:decoration-red-700" aria-label="Link to https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/on-small-business/microsoft-attack-blamed-on-china-morphs-into-global-crisis/2021/03/06/48bffec4-7ee1-11eb-8c5e-32e47b42b51b_story.html" target="_blank" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/on-small-business/microsoft-attack-blamed-on-china-morphs-into-global-crisis/2021/03/06/48bffec4-7ee1-11eb-8c5e-32e47b42b51b_story.html">sources</a>, including <a class="underline decoration-2 decoration-red-600 underline-offset-2 break-words hover:decoration-red-700" aria-label="Link to https://www.microsoft.com/security/blog/2021/03/02/hafnium-targeting-exchange-servers/" target="_blank" href="https://www.microsoft.com/security/blog/2021/03/02/hafnium-targeting-exchange-servers/">Microsoft</a>, have indicated that the HAFNIUM group is &quot;... state-sponsored and operating out of China ...&quot;</p><p> </p><p><strong>Who is Being Targeted?</strong></p><p>At this point, the targeting of HAFNIUM appears opportunistic. Indeed, since Microsoft released their initial blog, the actors <a class="underline decoration-2 decoration-red-600 underline-offset-2 break-words hover:decoration-red-700" aria-label="Link to https://krebsonsecurity.com/2021/03/at-least-30000-u-s-organizations-newly-hacked-via-holes-in-microsofts-email-software/" target="_blank" href="https://krebsonsecurity.com/2021/03/at-least-30000-u-s-organizations-newly-hacked-via-holes-in-microsofts-email-software/">have</a> &quot;... stepped up attacks on any vulnerable, unpatched Exchange servers (2013, 2016, and 2019) worldwide.&quot; The HAFNIUM group, though, has targeted organizations in the past.</p><p>As both Microsoft and FireEye mentioned, HAFNIUM is a group with a bit of track record. Microsoft identified that they have targeted several industries in the past. These included</p><ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 my-4 ml-4 xl:ml-6"><li class="">Infectious disease research</li><li class="">Law firms</li><li class="">Universities</li><li class="">Defense contractors</li><li class="">Think tanks</li><li class="">Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs)</li></ul><p>FireEye also mentioned that they had observed several industries affected by the new attack, including</p><ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 my-4 ml-4 xl:ml-6"><li class="">Retailers</li><li class="">Local Governments</li><li class="">Universities</li><li class="">Engineering Firms</li></ul><p>They also identified that there is possible related activity observed in Asia as well</p><ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 my-4 ml-4 xl:ml-6"><li class="">Federal governments</li><li class="">Telecommunications providers</li></ul><p> </p><p><strong>What Are The Actors Doing?</strong></p><p>Since early January, the actors have been exploiting several 0-day vulnerabilities in Exchange.
