data-toc-skip>Rare Earth Material Lure Delivering Shellcode Loader</h1><div class="post-meta text-muted"> <span> Posted <time data-ts="1764115200" data-df="ll" data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-placement="bottom" > Nov 26, 2025 </time> </span><div class="d-flex justify-content-between"> <span> By <em> <a href="https://github.com/dmpdump">dmpdump</a> </em> </span><div> <span class="readtime" data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-placement="bottom" title="670 words" > <em>3 min</em> read</span></div></div></div></header><div class="content"><p>On November 21, 2025, <a href="https://x.com/malwrhunterteam">Malware Hunter Team</a> shared an <a href="https://x.com/malwrhunterteam/status/1991875797762842913">interesting sample</a> on X, uploaded to VirusTotal from Singapore. The ZIP file in question is named <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">China’s Governance of Rare Earths and its Global Implications.zip</code>. It contains a password-protected PDF named <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">China’s Governance of Rare Earths and its Global Implications.pdf</code> and an executable named <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">SecurityKey.exe</code>. The lure is clear: the victim tries to open the PDF, and, when prompted for a password, they are led to running the executable to get the key to unprotect it.</p><p>Contents of the ZIP file:</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/content.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/content.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/pass.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/pass.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><h1 id="securitykey">SecurityKey</h1><p><code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">SecurityKey.exe</code> is a simple shellcode loader. When it is executed, a message box is displayed indicating the PDF password: <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">202511</code>. Using this password, we can see the content of the PDF: a paper from August 2025 named <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">China’s Governance of Rare Earths and its Global Implications</code>, published by the S. Rajaratnam School of International Studies (RSIS), a global graduate school and think tank focused on strategic studies and security affairs associated with the Nanyang Technological University of Singapore. This is consistent with the geography that the malware was submitted from.</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/pdf.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/pdf.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p>Immediately after the password is displayed, <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">SecurityKey.exe</code> allocates memory with <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">PAGE_EXECUTE_READWRITE</code> permissions. A shellcode embedded in the <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">.rdata</code> section of the executable is then copied to the newly allocated buffer and is executed by dereferencing a function pointer to it.</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/sc1.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/sc1.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p>The shellcode loaded by <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">SecurityKey.exe</code> is responsible for downloading and executing additional shellcode. This downloader shellcode, as is common in position-independent code, walks the Process Environment Block (PEB) to get the base address of <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">ntdll.dll</code>:</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/ntdllbase.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/ntdllbase.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p>Once the base address of <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">ntdll.dll</code> is located, it is used to retrieve additional functions using API hashing. Additionally, <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">wininet.dll</code> is loaded using <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">LoadLibrary</code>, and additional APIs from that module are also resolved via API hashing. The resolved APIs already gives an idea of the functionality of this shellcode:</p><ul><li>It will act as a downloader<li>It will use fibers</ul><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/apiresol1.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/apiresol1.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p>The API hashing algorithm used by this shellcode seems to be a modified version of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fowler%E2%80%93Noll%E2%80%93Vo_hash_function">Fowler–Noll–Vo (FNV) algorithm</a>, specifically <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">fvn-1a 32-bit</code>. The offset and prime constants seem to have been modified.</p><ul><li>Original offset: 2166136261<li><p>Original prime: 16777619</p><li>Modified offset: 3126136263<li>Modified prime: 16737619</ul><p>Function resolving APIs by hash:</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/apihash.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/apihash.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p>After the API resolutions, the shellcode uses <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">wininet.dll</code> functions to download additional shellcode from the <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">www.global-reia[.]com/image-directory/da.mp3</code>.</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/downloader.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/downloader.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p>The domain hosting the payload impersonates the legitimate <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">global-reia.org</code>, associated with the <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">Rare Earth Industry Association (REIA)</code>, a non-profit organization associated with topics related to Rare Earth Metals.</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/domain.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/domain.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p>Finally, another buffer with <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">PAGE_EXECUTE_READWRITE</code> permission is allocated, shellcode is downloaded to it and executed creating a fiber and switching to it via the <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">SwitchToFiber</code> API. A fiber is a unit of execution that must be manually scheduled by the application, as documented <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/processes-and-threads">here</a>. Shellcode execution via a fiber is a known technique. In my experience, it is not the most common way of executing shellcodes.</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/fiber.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/fiber.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p><a href="https://github.com/dmpdump/ShellcodeFiber/blob/main/main.cpp">Here</a> is a simple proof of concept for shellcode execution using a fiber that I wrote some time ago.</p><p>Unfortunately, the next stage shellcode downloaded from <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">global-reia[.]com</code> was no longer available at the time of analysis. The <code class="language-plaintext highlighter-rouge">global-reia[.]com</code> domain resolves to the following infrastructure:</p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/infra1.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/infra1.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><p><a href="/assets/images/reia/infra2.png" class="popup img-link shimmer"><img src="/assets/images/reia/infra2.png" alt="sshot" loading="lazy"></a></p><h1 id="final-notes">Final notes</h1><p>At this point, I do not have enough technical evidence to venture into attribution. <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare-earth_element">Rare earth materials</a> have a very high strategic value in today’s economy, especially due to its application in tech and the military, making its supply chain critical for economic development. Recently, the US and China reached an <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-strikes-deal-on-economic-and-trade-relations-with-china/">agreement</a> in which China would suspend the proposed export controls for rare earth materials. China is the top producer of rare earth materials, holding the largest reserves in the world. Earlier this year, Singapore <a href="https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/what-singapores-first-public-cyber-attribution-tells-us">publicly accused</a> China of cyber activity against their country, however, I could not correlate the loaders and infrastructure to any other China-nexus activity that I am aware of. Admittedly, my resources for attribution are very limited.</p><h1 id="iocs">IOCs</h1><ul><li>China’s Governance of Rare Earths and its Global Implications.zip:
