<p><b>Maksim Viktorovich Yakubets</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: Максим Викторович Якубец) is a Russian<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> computer expert and alleged computer hacker. He is alleged to have been a member of the <a href="/wiki/Evil_Corp" class="mw-redirect" title="Evil Corp">Evil Corp</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jabber_Zeus" title="Jabber Zeus">Jabber Zeus</a> Crew, as well as the alleged leader of the <a href="/wiki/Bugat_(Trojan_horse)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bugat (Trojan horse)">Bugat</a> malware conspiracy.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Russian media openly describe Yakubets as a "hacker who stole $100 million", friend of <a href="/wiki/Dmitry_Peskov" title="Dmitry Peskov">Dmitry Peskov</a> and discussed his lavish lifestyle, including luxury wedding with a daughter of FSB officer Eduard Bendersky and Lamborghini with "<i>ВОР</i>" (Russian for "thief") registration plate.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Yakubets's impunity in Russia is perceived as clue of his close ties with <a href="/wiki/Federal_Security_Service" title="Federal Security Service">FSB</a>, but also criticized by domestic information security experts such as <a href="/wiki/Ilya_Sachkov" title="Ilya Sachkov">Ilya Sachkov</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Indictments">Indictments</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maksim_Yakubets&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Indictments"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>On November 13, 2019, Yakubets was charged in the <a href="/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Pennsylvania" title="United States District Court for the Western District of Pennsylvania">United States District Court for the Western District of Pennsylvania</a> for allegedly conspiring in the development, maintenance, distribution, and infection of Bugat malware. The following day, he was charged in the <a href="/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_District_of_Nebraska" title="United States District Court for the District of Nebraska">United States District Court for the District of Nebraska</a> for his alleged involvement in the installation of <a href="/wiki/Zeus_(malware)" title="Zeus (malware)">Zeus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>9<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
