                    <div class="blog-post-content__section--text"><h2>Introduction</h2>
<p>In the summer of 2020, Group-IB specialists discovered a malware distribution campaign exploiting Telegram&#8217;s legitimate features. Analysis showed that the attackers used the technique to distribute Raccoon stealer, i.e. malware spread through the Malware-as-a-Service model on one of darknet forums. They, in particular, used Telegram channels in order to bypass blocking of active C&amp;C servers.</p>
<p>Raccoon Stealer collects system information, account data, bank card data, and autofill form details from browsers (Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Opera, etc.). What&#8217;s more, Raccoon Stealer scans the infected device for information about valid crypto wallets. If successful, it gains access to configuration files.</p>

The campaign was divided into four stages based on the tools used (type of malware, registrars for creating infrastructure, etc.):</p>

<p>Most domains related to the investigated campaign were registered with two registrars: <strong>Cloud2m</strong> and <strong>Host Africa</strong>. Cloud2m was used in earlier attacks. In mid-July 2020, some of these domains moved to Host Africa.</p>

<p>Group-IB experts concluded that the purpose of the campaign in question was to steal payment and user data. The attackers used several attack vectors and tools to deliver the malware.</p>
<p>It was also discovered that in early 2020, before distributing the Raccoon stealer, the attackers had distributed samples of another stealer called Vidar. To do so, they used attachments with malicious macros and phishing pages created with the Mephistophilus phishing kit.</p>
<div id="attachment_26565" style="width: 1210px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><picture><source srcset="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/3-min-28.png.webp" type="image/webp"><img aria-describedby="caption-attachment-26565" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-26565 webpexpress-processed" src="https://www.group-ib.com/wp-content/uploads/3-min-28.png" alt="Infection pattern in the malicious campaigns of hacker group FakeSecurity" width="1200" height="675"></picture><p id="caption-attachment-26565" class="wp-caption-text">Infection pattern in the malicious campaigns of hacker group FakeSecurity</p></div>
<p>This malware distribution technique reminded Group-IB experts of the pattern used by FakeSecurity JS-sniffer operators during the campaign <a href="https://www.group-ib.com/blog/fakesecurity/">described</a> in November 2019. Apart from having similar toolkits, both series of attacks targeted e-commerce. In May 2020, Group-IB identified online stores that had been infected with a modified JS-sniffer of the FakeSecurity family. The JS-sniffer was obfuscated using the <strong>aaencode</strong> algorithm, while the domains used to store the code and collect stolen bank card data were registered during the second wave with the same registrars as the domains that we discovered while investigating the malicious campaign. As such, it can be assumed that FakeSecurity JS-sniffer operators were behind the stealer distribution campaign.</p>

<h2>Relation to FakeSecurity</h2>
<p><strong>This malicious campaign bears a striking resemblance to a series of FakeSecurity JS-sniffer attacks</strong> described by Group-IB in November 2019. Past attacks targeted owners of online stores powered by Magento CMS. In the campaign <a href="/blog/fakesecurity/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">described</a> previously, the attackers also used such tools as the Vidar stealer and the Mephistophilus phishing kit, with an identical template for Adobe updates. In addition, the attackers used the same hosting service to register domains in both campaigns.</p>
<p><strong>In the 2020 campaign, the same attack vector was used and involved subsequent distribution of the Raccoon stealer.</strong> In addition, the investigation revealed messages sent to several online stores from <a href="/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection" class="__cf_email__" data-cfemail="b2d0d7c8d1dd9cc3c7dbc1d7838b8a8af2c5c29cc2de">[email&#160;protected]</a> and <a href="/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection" class="__cf_email__" data-cfemail="204f55545449410e4c454e45111918156057500e504c">[email&#160;protected]</a>.</p>

        let sidebarHeight = sidebar.offsetHeight;
        let windowHeight = document.documentElement.clientHeight;
        if (windowHeight < (sidebarHeight + 48)) {
            let sidebarAuthors = sidebar.querySelector('.sidebar__authors');
            let sidebarMorePosts = sidebar.querySelector('.sidebar__more-posts');
            let authorsHeight = sidebarAuthors ? sidebarAuthors.offsetHeight : 0;
            let morePostsHeight = sidebarMorePosts ? sidebarMorePosts.offsetHeight : 0;
            let sidebarTop = authorsHeight + morePostsHeight + 140;
            jQuery(sidebar).css('top', -sidebarTop + 'px');
        } else {
            jQuery(sidebar).css('top', '72px');
        }
    }
