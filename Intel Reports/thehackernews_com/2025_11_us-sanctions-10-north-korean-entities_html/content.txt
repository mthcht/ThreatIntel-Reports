<p>The U.S. Treasury Department on Tuesday imposed sanctions against eight individuals and two entities within North Korea's global financial network for laundering money for <a href="https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/newsroom/news/2741814-2741814" rel="noopener" target="_blank">various illicit schemes</a>, including cybercrime and <a href="https://thehackernews.com/2025/08/us-treasury-sanctions-dprk-it-worker.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">information technology (IT) worker fraud</a>.</p>
<p>"North Korean state-sponsored hackers steal and launder money to fund the regime's nuclear weapons program," <a href="https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0302" rel="noopener" target="_blank">said</a> Under Secretary of the Treasury for Terrorism and Financial Intelligence John K. Hurley.</p>
<p>"By generating revenue for Pyongyang's weapons development, these actors directly threaten U.S. and global security. The Treasury will continue to pursue the facilitators and enablers behind these schemes to cut off the DPRK's illicit revenue streams."</p>
<div class='dog_two clear'><center class='cf'><a href='https://thehackernews.uk/zz--inside-d' rel='nofollow noopener sponsored' target='_blank'><img class='lazyload' alt="DFIR Retainer Services" src="data:image/png;base64,iVBORw0KGgoAAAANSUhEUgAAAAEAAAABCAQAAAC1HAwCAAAAC0lEQVR42mP8Xw8AAoMBgDTD2qgAAAAASUVORK5CYII=" data-src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEg-X-E4jERutAGUVjhgWkMe8ShYj1nqWzq1SqeBFJikipPF-xT70YnaSLlaR3UkhNP8Q47kifLbAEGXpknthRo_lfqDawf05df5ozIbkBTrupYoGXl8axiISZxIKgHQ8GOm47oYIZ9l8PnjNrSw_NpS6YzSZzvy0bhoqKd91EG3BcIs3jONhIjeB4iXl-dY/s738-e100/zz--inside-d.png" width="729" height="91"/></a></center></div>
<p>The names of <a href="https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20251104" rel="noopener" target="_blank">sanctioned individuals and entities</a> are listed below -</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Jang Kuk Chol (Jang) and Ho Jong Son</strong>, who are said to have helped manage funds, including $5.3 million in cryptocurrency, on behalf of First Credit Bank (aka Cheil Credit Bank), which was <a href="https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sm0165" rel="noopener" target="_blank">previously subjected to sanctions</a> in September 2017 for facilitating North Korea's missile programs</li>
<li><strong>Korea Mangyongdae Computer Technology Company</strong> (KMCTC), an IT company based in North Korea that has dispatched two IT worker delegations to the Chinese cities of Shenyang and Dandong, and has used Chinese nationals as banking proxies to conceal the origin of funds generated as part of the fraudulent employment scheme</li>
<li><strong>U Yong Su</strong>, KMCTC's current president</li>
<li><strong>Ryujong Credit Bank</strong>, which has provided financial assistance in sanctions avoidance activities between China and North Korea</li>
<li><strong>Ho Yong Chol, Han Hong Gil, Jong Sung Hyok, Choe Chun Pom, and Ri Jin Hyok</strong>, who are representatives of North Korean financial institutions in Russia and China and are said to have facilitated transactions worth millions of dollars on behalf of the sanctioned banks</li>
</ul>
<p>A portion of $5.3 million has been linked to a North Korean ransomware actor known to have targeted U.S. victims in the past and handled revenue from IT worker operations. </p>

<p>Describing North Korean cyber actors as orchestrating espionage, disruptive attacks, and financial theft at a scale "unmatched" by any other country, the Treasury said the Pyongyang-affiliated cybercriminals have stolen over $3 billion, mostly in digital assets, over the past three years using sophisticated malware and social engineering.</p>
<p>The department also accused the regime of leveraging its IT army located across the world to gain employment at companies by obfuscating their nationality and identities, and funneling back a huge chunk of their income back to the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK).</p>
<div class='dog_two clear'><center class='cf'><a href='https://thehackernews.uk/endpoint-protect-d' rel='nofollow noopener sponsored' target='_blank'><img class='lazyload' alt="CIS Build Kits" src="data:image/png;base64,iVBORw0KGgoAAAANSUhEUgAAAAEAAAABCAQAAAC1HAwCAAAAC0lEQVR42mP8Xw8AAoMBgDTD2qgAAAAASUVORK5CYII=" data-src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEi_HDUlvbnYoGKWPtfjTL83IuUQ73P6igcLnrsVdRjU2sVKjgD6kcX1QX3psmxbKnQ-GfeZ5xCNgnJF64an1ZS4C9mxkNAfVoSaNVMTGdlCMv4nchoUdLGCDbdB6BQWiRScizTQjINO1DArRQdcgzs5CbapyRNjTVLX_RBZ4eS9Kg3iB7UkazuU0e5YlPLZ/s728-e100/sem-d.png" width="729" height="91"/></a></center></div>
<p>"In some instances, DPRK IT workers engage other foreign freelance programmers to establish business partnerships," it added. "They collaborate with these non-North Korean freelance workers on projects which were originally commissioned to those workers and split the revenue."</p>
<p>According to TRM Labs, the cryptocurrency wallet addresses linked to First Credit Bank show "consistent inbound flows resembling salary payments" and that "these flows likely represent income from IT workers employed abroad under false identities."</p>
<p>In all, the wallets controlled by the bank are said to have received more than $12.7 million between June 2023 and May 2025, indicating sustained activity spanning over two years.</p>
<p>"Together, these individuals and entities form a central component of Pyongyang's sanctions-evasion architecture, enabling the regime to move millions of dollars through both traditional and digital channels, including cryptocurrency, to fund weapons programs and cyber operations," the blockchain intelligence firm <a href="https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/us-treasury-sanctions-dprk-bankers-and-front-companies-laundering-proceeds-from-cybercrime-and-it-worker-operations" rel="noopener" target="_blank">said</a>.</p>
