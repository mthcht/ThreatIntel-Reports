                            <h1 class="title-post" itemprop="name">Forensics of Operating System Agentic AI Activity Traces [Part 2]</h1>
                    <div class="date">
            <span itemprop="datePublished"><a href="https://threat.boutique/archive/2026-03" title="Show all posts made on this month">15 March 2026</a></span>

        </div>
                    <div class="featured-image">
                <a href="https://threat.boutique/2026/03/forensics-of-operating-system-agentic-ai-activity-traces-part-2"><img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260315165342-image.jpg" alt="Forensics of Operating System Agentic AI Activity Traces [Part 2]"/></a>
            </div>
                                        <div class="post-body post-1773579171" itemprop="articleBody">
            <p><strong>Background:</strong></p>

<p>During our previous article, we discovered the steps to reveal the root cause of the incident that involved the Claude AI agentic desktop.
Over this article we shall discuss google gemini cli .</p>

<p>Google Gemini Cli:
Because Google Gemini is an NPM package, we can assume that the parent process for some actions should be Node.js.
<img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260315165759-Screenshot_20260315_140734.png" alt="enter image description here" /></p>

<p>Google Gemini CLI stores all of its configuration files inside a special folder for Windows, which is located at CurrentUser/.gemini</p>

<p><img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260315165913-1.png" alt="enter image description here" /></p>

<p>Having said that, during forensic activities we can also recover the chat history from C:\Users\CurrentUser&#46;gemini\tmp\CurrentUser\chats.
<img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260315170351-301.png" alt="enter image description here" /></p>

<p>Because of the nature of this specific file, it is not easy to detect—even when using Sysmon or other classic event tracers. In such situations, you need to tune your own EDR to capture these types of logs.</p>

<p><strong>Conclusion:</strong>
The <code>.gemini</code> folder, particularly the contents within its <code>tmp</code> subdirectory, can yield valuable forensic evidence such as chat histories. However, due to the inconspicuous nature of these files, standard tools like Sysmon may not suffice to detect relevant activity. Therefore, it is important to customize your EDR solutions to monitor and log interactions within these specific directories.</p>
