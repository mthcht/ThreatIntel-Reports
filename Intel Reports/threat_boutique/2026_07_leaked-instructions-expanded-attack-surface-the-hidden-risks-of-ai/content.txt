                            <h1 class="title-post" itemprop="name">Leaked Instructions, Expanded Attack Surface: The Hidden Risks of Artificial Intelligence</h1>
                    <div class="date">
            <span itemprop="datePublished"><a href="https://threat.boutique/archive/2026-07" title="Show all posts made on this month">12 July 2026</a></span>
            - Posted in            <span itemprop="articleSection"><a href="https://threat.boutique/category/threat-analyze">Threat Analyze</a></span> by            <span itemprop="author"><a href="https://threat.boutique/author/b3vSclAuunBMsdt">Jok3r</a></span> -
            <span><a href="https://threat.boutique/2026/07/leaked-instructions-expanded-attack-surface-the-hidden-risks-of-ai" rel="permalink">Permalink</a></span>
        </div>
                    <div class="featured-image">
                <a href="https://threat.boutique/2026/07/leaked-instructions-expanded-attack-surface-the-hidden-risks-of-ai"><img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260712124636-q1.jpg" alt="Leaked Instructions, Expanded Attack Surface: The Hidden Risks of Artificial Intelligence"/></a>
            </div>
                                        <div class="post-body post-1783845953" itemprop="articleBody">
            <p><strong>Background:</strong>
When using any AI model, vendors implement guardrails to prevent abusive use and to ensure the model operates within its intended boundaries. These guardrails help block harmful, unsafe, or unauthorized actions and complement the safety mechanisms incorporated into the model during training.</p>

<p>In many cases, these guardrails take the form of instructions or prompts that define the model's purpose, expected behavior, and operational constraints. They provide guidance to the model on how it should respond to user requests and what actions it should avoid, helping ensure that the AI system functions as intended.</p>

<p><strong>Way of Abuse:</strong>
Let's imagine we have a backend system where, for each user prompt, it automatically adds its own instructions to the request before sending it to the AI model.
<img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260712133336-03.png" alt="enter image description here" /></p>

<p>In the first attempt, we were not able to retrieve any data. However, during the second attempt, the chatbot returned the hidden instructions as part of its response.
<img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260712133722-32.png" alt="enter image description here" /></p>

<p>Prevention steps:
1. Never expose secrets in instruction and store them in vault .
2. Put DLP on model layer .</p>

<p><strong>Conclusion:</strong>
The primary security concern is not the disclosure of the prompt itself, but the sensitive information, business logic, permissions, or security assumptions that may be embedded within it. Organizations should design AI systems with the assumption that instructions can become visible and ensure that critical security controls are enforced outside the model.</p>

<p>By separating secrets from prompts, implementing external authorization mechanisms, applying data loss prevention controls, and continuously monitoring AI interactions, organizations can reduce the impact of system prompt leakage and build more resilient AI applications.</p>
