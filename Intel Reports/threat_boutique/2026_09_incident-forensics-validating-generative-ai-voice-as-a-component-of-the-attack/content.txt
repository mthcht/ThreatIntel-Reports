                            <h1 class="title-post" itemprop="name">Incident Forensics: Validating Generative AI Voice as a Component of the Attack</h1>
                    <div class="date">
            <span itemprop="datePublished"><a href="https://threat.boutique/archive/2026-09" title="Show all posts made on this month">19 September 2026</a></span>
            - Posted in            <span itemprop="articleSection"><a href="https://threat.boutique/category/incident-response">Incident Response</a></span> by            <span itemprop="author"><a href="https://threat.boutique/author/b3vSclAuunBMsdt">Jok3r</a></span> -
            <span><a href="https://threat.boutique/2026/09/incident-forensics-validating-generative-ai-voice-as-a-component-of-the-attack" rel="permalink">Permalink</a></span>
        </div>
                    <div class="featured-image">
                <a href="https://threat.boutique/2026/09/incident-forensics-validating-generative-ai-voice-as-a-component-of-the-attack"><img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260919173905-123.png" alt="Incident Forensics: Validating Generative AI Voice as a Component of the Attack"/></a>
            </div>
                                        <div class="post-body post-1789824969" itemprop="articleBody">
            <p><strong>Background:</strong></p>

<p>As AI  capabilities continue to evolve, threat actors have also started leveraging generative AI tools as part of their attacks. One well known scenario that comes to mind is the use of generative AI to impersonate a company CEO or another well-known team member. Attackers could use AI-generated voice or other synthetic media to convince employees to grant access to company infrastructure, disclose sensitive information, or perform fraudulent actions. And in case of success you can face with incident .</p>

<p><strong>Attack Scenario:</strong></p>

<p>Attacker is scraping known person voice from public speech, over call recording . Then via that information converting converting any text to speech . Right after that calling to customer support or company phone number .</p>

<p><strong>Detection Method:</strong></p>

<p>Preventing this kind of attack is not an easy task. However, if an incident has already occurred and you have access to recordings or a large database of historical records, AI can be used again to analyze the data and identify signs of AI-generated or manipulated content. This can help investigators determine whether synthetic voice or other generative AI techniques were involved in the attack.</p>

<p><strong>Analyze method and toolset implementation:</strong></p>

<p>To implement it you need  only have DeepFake/GenAI/Synthetic voice detection model + Simple Backed + UI (Optional) .</p>

<p><strong>Implementation result:</strong></p>

<p>Built upon the methodology of our proprietary tool, this complex solution reliably detected synthetic versus authentic voice audio.
<img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260919175332-qwe.png" alt="enter image description here" /></p>

<p><strong>Prevention Steps:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Organize awareness training.</li>
<li>Implement custom forensic tools with custom models to maximize effectiveness.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Disadvantages:</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>For non-English languages, you must train your own custom model.</li>
<li>In some edge cases, it may generate false positives, so continuous
fine-tuning is required.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Conclusion:</strong>
As we move deeper into the AI era, we must adopt a new mindset and toolset. The environment has fundamentally changed, and our legacy approaches are no longer enough to keep up with synthetic threats.</p>
