                            <h1 class="title-post" itemprop="name">The Model Is Fine. The Author Is Gone.</h1>
                    <div class="date">

                                        <div class="post-body post-1788620660" itemprop="articleBody">
            <h4><strong>Background:</strong></h4>

<p>The threat landscape with introduction  AI has changed, and at this moment we also need to focus on an additional threat: LLM supply-chain attacks that abuse its name and capabilities.</p>

<h4><strong>Focus areas:</strong></h4>

<p>From time to time, we can leverage open-source LLM models from open registries . 
But with all this beauty, these models can contain hidden threats - some of which are visible, while others are not.
Visible threats include cases where a model somehow contains a malicious file, or when the model’s author replaces it with a poisoned one.
An invisible threat is when the model is poisoned by default.</p>

<p>Here are some points which  trigger about risky model:</p>

<ul>
<li>Repo takeover / handle re-registration</li>
<li>Silent artefact swap</li>
<li>Pickle deserialization RCE</li>
<li>trust_remote_code execution</li>
<li>Dependency hijacking</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Detection method:</strong>
To detect such models used in your environment, you first need to create an inventory of where you are sourcing your models from. Then, if they are public, you should write your own scanner that will check them and perform scanning when required. It can also be integrated into your CI/CD pipeline.</p>

<p>Example of such self developed LLM registry base .
<img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260905194010-123.png" alt="enter image description here" /></p>

<h4><strong>Conclusion</strong></h4>

<p>The rise of open-source LLMs from public registries like has expanded the AI threat landscape with supply-chain attacks that can be either visible (malicious files, artefact swaps, repo takeovers) or invisible (pre-poisoned models). Effective protection starts with thorough model inventory, followed by custom scanners that can be embedded in CI/CD pipelines to continuously detect and mitigate these risks before models enter production environments.</p>
