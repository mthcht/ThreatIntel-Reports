                            <h1 class="title-post" itemprop="name">XSS Meets AI: The Invisible Attack the Victim Never Sees</h1>
                    <div class="date">
            <span itemprop="datePublished"><a href="https://threat.boutique/archive/2026-09" title="Show all posts made on this month">26 September 2026</a></span>
            - Posted in            <span itemprop="articleSection"><a href="https://threat.boutique/category/threat-analyze">Threat Analyze</a></span> by            <span itemprop="author"><a href="https://threat.boutique/author/b3vSclAuunBMsdt">Jok3r</a></span> -
            <span><a href="https://threat.boutique/2026/09/xss-meets-ai-the-invisible-attack-the-victim-never-sees" rel="permalink">Permalink</a></span>
        </div>
                    <div class="featured-image">
                <a href="https://threat.boutique/2026/09/xss-meets-ai-the-invisible-attack-the-victim-never-sees"><img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260926234050-ULed7.jpg" alt="XSS Meets AI: The Invisible Attack the Victim Never Sees"/></a>
            </div>
                                        <div class="post-body post-1790451463" itemprop="articleBody">
            <p><strong><em>Background:</em></strong></p>

<p>Nowadays, AI-powered features have become increasingly common. With just one click, users can summarize the content of an entire page, making these features extremely time-saving and convenient.
However, this convenience introduces another potential security concern:</p>

<p>what happens when an old technique such as <strong>stored XSS</strong> is combined with an AI-powered feature?</p>

<p>This combination could create new security challenges, potentially allowing malicious content stored on a page to influence how AI processes or summarizes that content.</p>

<p><strong><em>Attack scenario:</em></strong></p>

<p>Attacker is exposing stored XSS on the page and storing it for other user
<img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260926234824-44.png" alt="enter image description here" />
After the victim clicks on the button (Summarize with AI or page content summarize), the stored payload can trigger an non visible  context/instruction  to the AI model behind the scenes. This request may not be visible to the victim or to other users who are using the same board for communication.
<img src="https://threat.boutique/content/images/20260926235229-55.png" alt="enter image description here" /></p>

<p><strong><em>Prevention steps:</em></strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Always perform penetration testing on your product before every release to identify and prevent XSS vulnerabilities.</li>
<li>Even when communication with an AI model goes through a trusted chain, always implement DLP controls on both the input and output of the model.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong><em>Conclusion:</em></strong>
Traditional vulnerabilities such as stored XSS can create new and less visible attack paths. Security teams should therefore treat AI features as part of the application’s attack surface, combining strong input/output controls with regular security testing to reduce the risk of silent abuse.</p>
