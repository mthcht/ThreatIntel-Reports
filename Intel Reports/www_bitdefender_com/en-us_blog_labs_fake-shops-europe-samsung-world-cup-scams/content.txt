      </p></div> <!----> <!----> <!----></div></div> <div class="tw-mt-4 sm:tw-mt-0 sm:tw-w-2/3"><div><div class="tw-animate-pulse"><div class="tw-h-10 tw-w-full tw-rounded tw-bg-[#e1e3e5]"></div></div></div></div></div> <picture class="tw-my-6"><source data-not-lazy="" media="(max-width: 600px)" data-srcset="https://blogapp.bitdefender.com/labs/content/images/size/w600/2026/06/ChatGPT-Image-Jun-23--2026--01_03_09-PM.png"> <source data-not-lazy="" media="(min-width: 701px)" data-srcset="https://blogapp.bitdefender.com/labs/content/images/size/w1000/2026/06/ChatGPT-Image-Jun-23--2026--01_03_09-PM.png"> <img data-not-lazy="" data-src="https://blogapp.bitdefender.com/labs/content/images/size/w600/2026/06/ChatGPT-Image-Jun-23--2026--01_03_09-PM.png" alt="Fake shops target shoppers across Europe with fake Samsung deals, counterfeit goods and World Cup scams" title="Fake shops target shoppers across Europe with fake Samsung deals, counterfeit goods and World Cup scams" width="600" height="400" src="https://blogapp.bitdefender.com/labs/content/images/size/w600/2026/06/ChatGPT-Image-Jun-23--2026--01_03_09-PM.png" class="tw-my-4 tw-w-full"></picture> <div class="content tw-mb-12 tw-text-lg tw-text-black"><p>Cybercriminals are scaling fake online stores into a coordinated multinational business.</p><p>A Bitdefender Labs investigation identified more than 55 fake-shop campaigns targeting consumers across 12 European countries between March and May 2026. The campaigns mimicked some of the world’s most recognizable brands, including Samsung, Nike, Adidas, ZARA, H&amp;M, Amazon, Lidl, and SHEIN.</p><p>Attackers used Facebook ads, WhatsApp messages, email campaigns, SMS messages, phone calls and fraudulent websites to lure victims into making payments, sharing personal information or buying fake goods.</p><p>Bitdefender researchers mapped more than 40 domains associated with these operations and observed repeat tactics that linked campaigns across multiple countries. Some operators relied on rotating domains and misleading redirects. Others built counterfeit supply chains through WhatsApp and password-protected catalogs. Several campaigns exploited anticipation surrounding the 2026 FIFA World Cup.</p><p>The findings show that fake-shop operations have evolved far beyond isolated scam websites. Many now resemble professional e-commerce businesses with advertising budgets, localization strategies and infrastructure designed to evade detection.</p><h2 id="key-takeaways">Key takeaways</h2><ul>
<li>Researchers identified more than 55 confirmed fake-shop campaigns targeting consumers across Europe.</li>
<li>Attackers impersonated Samsung, Nike, Adidas, ZARA, H&amp;M, Amazon, Lidl, SHEIN and other major brands.</li>
<li>Social media ads and WhatsApp emerged as the most-abused channels.</li>
<li>Several campaigns exploited FIFA World Cup 2026 excitement.</li>
<li>Operators reused infrastructure across countries and brands.</li>
<li>Redirect chains, Unicode lookalike domains, domain rotation, and localized content helped attackers evade detection.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="by-the-numbers">By the numbers</h2><blockquote>
<ul>
<li>55+ confirmed fake-shop campaigns.</li>
<li>15+ impersonated brands.</li>
<li>12 European countries targeted.</li>
<li>40+ fake domains mapped.</li>
<li>6 attack channels observed.</li>
</ul>
</blockquote>
<p>The investigation tracked coordinated campaigns operating between <strong>March and May 2026</strong> across social media platforms, WhatsApp, email, SMS, phone calls, and fraudulent e-commerce websites.</p><h2 id="fake-shop-campaigns-spread-across-europe">Fake-shop campaigns spread across Europe</h2><p>Bitdefender security researchers have observed confirmed activity across the EU and the United Kingdom, including Germany, France, Italy, Poland, Spain, the Netherlands, Sweden, Portugal, Austria, Ireland and Romania.</p><p>The campaigns appeared on multiple channels:</p><ul>
<li>Social media advertisements</li>
<li>WhatsApp messages</li>
<li>Email campaigns</li>
<li>SMS campaigns</li>
<li>Phone calls</li>
<li>Dedicated fake e-commerce websites</li>
</ul>
<p>Rather than relying on a single fraudulent storefront, many operators rotated domains, changed brands, and adapted messaging to local audiences.</p><h2 id="social-media-ads-impersonate-trusted-brands">Social media ads impersonate trusted brands</h2><h3 id="samsung-galaxy-s26-ultra-offered-at-90-discounts">Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra offered at 90% discounts</h3><p>One campaign promoted Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra devices for only €249 while advertising discounts of up to 90%.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://blogapp.bitdefender.com/labs/content/images/2026/06/samsung.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="468" height="468"></figure><p>The operation targeted German consumers through Facebook advertisements and used domains such as:</p><ul>
<li>shopintertec[.]com</li>
<li>shop.zppzddw[.]shop</li>
<li>shop.fjjfxxz[.]shop</li>
</ul>
<p>Researchers found a mix of unrelated advertising personas promoting the same fraudulent offer. The campaign generated substantial engagement, including hundreds of thousands of ad views.</p><p>Researchers also saw a Netherlands-based variant that abused KPN branding. According to the investigation, ads promised Samsung-related offers through what appeared to be KPN-affiliated promotions, then redirected users to the fraudulent storefront crowndistrictstore[.]com.</p><h3 id="zara-and-nike-campaigns-shared-the-same-infrastructure">ZARA and Nike campaigns shared the same infrastructure</h3><p>Researchers also identified a Polish hub operating through notcia[.]shop that impersonated both ZARA and Nike.</p><p>The ZARA campaign advertised Italian fashion products and seasonal collections while directing shoppers to fake storefronts at the same time. The Nike campaign promoted apparel and requested detailed delivery information, including home addresses and postal codes.</p><p>Researchers also linked related domains to the operation, including:</p><ul>
<li>nebularaless[.]info</li>
<li>shryxla-mynx[.]icu</li>
<li>isblkx[.]shop</li>
</ul>
<p>Both campaigns used the same domain, notcia.shop, and the same advertising identity, suggesting an operational link between the ZARA and Nike impersonation activities. The Nike campaign also requested detailed delivery information, including house numbers and postal codes.</p><p>Moreover, these campaigns seek to gather personal information from their targets. The website states the following (translated from Polish):</p><blockquote>
<p>“Please provide your exact delivery address (including street address, house number, and postal code), otherwise we cannot guarantee product delivery! Thank you for your cooperation! Processing time: Ships within 48-72 hours of payment. Money-back guarantee.”</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="fake-hm-sales-targeted-italian-shoppers">Fake H&amp;M sales targeted Italian shoppers</h3><p>Another campaign promoted heavily discounted clothing through cyberloria[.]cfd.</p><p>This operation primarily targeted consumers in Italy and used Facebook advertisements that imitated legitimate H&amp;M promotions. Attackers exploited brand recognition and discount claims to drive traffic toward a suspicious domain unrelated to the actual retailer.</p><h2 id="whatsapp-becomes-a-counterfeit-marketplace">WhatsApp becomes a counterfeit marketplace</h2><h3 id="the-%E2%80%9Csupplier-carl%E2%80%9D-counterfeit-network">The “Supplier Carl” counterfeit network</h3><p>Bitdefender researchers also uncovered a China-based operator calling himself “Carl” who contacted European users through WhatsApp. The messages promoted “1:1 quality” counterfeit products, including Nike, Adidas, Jordan, Golden Goose, various watches, luxury accessories and much more.</p><p>Victims were directed toward password-protected Yupoo catalogs, including:</p><ul>
<li>carl-album.x.yupoo[.]com</li>
<li>adidas555.x.yupoo[.]com</li>
<li>golden-goose.x.yupoo[.]com</li>
<li>xy666999.x.yupoo[.]com</li>
</ul>
<p>The messages indicated that the operator offers DHL shipping to Europe and researchers observed the same infrastructure being promoted to users in Germany, France, and Italy.</p><p>Here is an example of such a message (original text): </p><blockquote>
<p>“Nice to meet you, my name is Carl, I am a supplier from China. I specialize in 1:1 quality, I have shoes, clothes, bags, belts, watches, sunglasses and other products. About shipping. The express delivery for European countries is DHL, the shipping time is about 7-12 days. The express delivery for other countries is FedEx and the shipping time is 7 days. Here are some of my yupoo products. Please click to view. If you like the item, please send me a picture and I will tell you the price.”</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="adidas-world-cup-fan-kit-scams">Adidas World Cup Fan-Kit scams</h3><p>Another campaign targeted German users with messages promising free Adidas Deutschland 2026 Fan Kits.</p><p>The operation distributed thousands of WhatsApp messages and relied on redirect infrastructure such as:</p><ul>
<li>linkrdr[.]cc</li>
<li>rewardpillar[.]cc</li>
<li>dealgo[.]cc</li>
<li>offerpilot[.]cc</li>
<li>msgdeal[.]cc</li>
</ul>
<p>The campaign exploited enthusiasm for the FIFA World Cup to convince recipients that they had won exclusive promotional merchandise.</p><h3 id="unicode-lookalike-domains-bypass-traditional-checks">Unicode lookalike domains bypass traditional checks</h3><p>Researchers also identified campaigns that abused visually deceptive domains—a surprisingly common tactic. Not everyone checks the actual URLs of the websites they open, and attackers know this all too well.</p><p>Examples included:</p><ul>
<li>adidas[.]com</li>
<li>adidas[.]com</li>
<li>adidȧs[.]com</li>
<li>ṇike[.]com</li>
<li>niḳe[.]com</li>
<li>rołex[.]com</li>
</ul>
<p>These domains appeared legitimate to human users while remaining technically different from authentic brand domains. The campaigns typically promised free shoes, watches, coupons, or anniversary gifts.</p><h2 id="email-sms-and-phone-scams-expand-the-ecosystem">Email, SMS, and phone scams expand the ecosystem</h2><h3 id="shein-themed-email-campaigns">SHEIN-themed email campaigns</h3><p>Security researchers also identified a large-scale email operation impersonating SHEIN order-confirmation messages.</p><p>The campaign relied on domains including:</p><ul>
<li>sheinnotice[.]com</li>
<li>sheinemail[.]com</li>
</ul>
<p>The attackers tried to make the messages look authentic through localized European content and retailer-themed branding. One unusual indicator involved legal documentation hosted on Google Drive rather than official websites.</p><h3 id="counterfeit-world-cup-merchandise-stores">Counterfeit World Cup merchandise stores</h3><p>Researchers also observed counterfeit merchandise campaigns exploiting FIFA World Cup 2026 interest.</p><p>Associated domains included:</p><ul>
<li>footballiscrazy[.]com</li>
<li>unitedfutballjersey[.]com</li>
<li>kicksfireshoes.com[.]co</li>
</ul>
<p>The operators promoted jerseys and football merchandise through email and SMS campaigns targeting European fans.</p><h3 id="amazon-clone-sites-and-subscription-traps">Amazon clone sites and subscription traps</h3><p>Amazon branding emerged as one of the most frequently observed in terms of impersonation attempts during the investigation.</p><p>Researchers identified:</p><ul>
<li>feasino[.]shop</li>
